# Russian-Linked Hackers Target Signal Users and Other Messaging Apps

According to available reporting, recent reports indicate that a group of hackers linked to Russian interests is conducting phishing attacks aimed at users of various messaging applications, including Signal. Identified by Microsoft as part of a cluster named Storm-2372, this emerging threat raises significant concerns about the security of digital communications and the potential for account hijacking.

## Overview of the Threat

Microsoft attributes a series of cyber attacks to the threat actor known as Storm-2372, which has been active since August 2024. This group reportedly employs a technique called "device code phishing" to compromise accounts on messaging platforms such as Signal, WhatsApp, and Microsoft Teams. Unlike traditional attacks that exploit technical vulnerabilities, these phishing attempts rely on social engineering, manipulating user behavior to gain access to accounts ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/02/microsoft-russian-linked-hackers-using.html), [ABC News](https://abcnews4.com/topic/Russian-linked)). The FBI has corroborated these findings, specifically warning that the phishing scams target Signal users, with the goal of hijacking their accounts ([ABC45](https://abc45.com/news/nation-world/gallery/russian-hacking-signal-fbi-warning-phishing-campaign-cybersecurity-messaging-apps), [ABC News](https://abcnews4.com/topic/Russian-linked)).

The implications of these phishing attacks are particularly concerning for users of secure messaging applications like Signal, which are often used for sensitive communications. The targeting of Signal users indicates that even platforms known for strong encryption are vulnerable to social engineering tactics ([ABC News](https://abcnews4.com/topic/Russian-linked)).

## Broader Impact

The threat actor has been observed targeting a wide range of sectors, including government, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and technology services. This strategic approach aims to undermine trust in digital communication channels, potentially leading to far-reaching consequences for organizational security and public confidence in digital privacy ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/02/microsoft-russian-linked-hackers-using.html)).

As of early 2025, the Storm-2372 threat cluster continues to evolve, with Microsoft actively monitoring its activities. The group's involvement in various cyber attacks underscores the persistent nature of this threat. Both the FBI's warnings and Microsoft's analysis highlight the urgency for users to remain vigilant against phishing attempts, particularly given the sophisticated tactics employed by these hackers ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/02/microsoft-russian-linked-hackers-using.html), [ABC45](https://abc45.com/news/nation-world/gallery/russian-hacking-signal-fbi-warning-phishing-campaign-cybersecurity-messaging-apps)).

## Recommendations for Users

Users of messaging applications should be aware of ongoing phishing threats and take proactive measures to protect their accounts. The FBI's emphasis on the exploitation of user behavior rather than technical vulnerabilities suggests that user education and awareness are critical in mitigating these risks ([ABC News](https://abcnews4.com/topic/Russian-linked), [ABC45](https://abc45.com/news/nation-world/gallery/russian-hacking-signal-fbi-warning-phishing-campaign-cybersecurity-messaging-apps)).
