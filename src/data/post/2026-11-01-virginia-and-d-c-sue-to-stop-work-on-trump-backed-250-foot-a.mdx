Virginia and Washington, D.C. officials have filed lawsuits seeking to block any work on a proposed monument associated with former President Donald Trump that would rise about 250 feet over the nation’s capital, according to a report from CBS News published May 10.

The new cases bring the total to three lawsuits aimed at halting potential construction of the planned arch in the Washington, D.C. area, CBS News reported, setting up a legal test over who can authorize and regulate a large new structure on or near the capital’s skyline.

## What the lawsuits seek

CBS News reported that the District of Columbia and the Commonwealth of Virginia each filed suit asking a court to stop any preparatory or construction work on the proposed arch. The outlet described the monument as a roughly 250-foot structure planned to rise over Washington, D.C.

The complaints, as summarized by CBS News, ask for a halt to potential work rather than responding to an active construction site. That indicates the jurisdictions are moving preemptively to clarify whether the project can proceed under existing laws and approvals.

With three lawsuits now pending, the legal filings collectively seek to prevent the project from advancing while courts review the authority for such a monument and any required oversight, CBS News reported.

## Where the Senate fits in

CBS News reported that the dispute has drawn in federal institutions, including the U.S. Senate, which has a role in overseeing or responding to major changes affecting federal property and the capital region. The report did not detail specific Senate actions tied to these lawsuits but identified the chamber as one of the entities involved in the broader debate over the proposed arch.

Because the capital region includes federal land, local governments, and interstate boundaries, CBS News noted that multiple levels of government are implicated when a large new monument is proposed. The involvement of the Senate places the controversy within a federal decision-making framework, alongside the legal challenges brought by Virginia and D.C.

## Why Virginia and D.C. are going to court

According to CBS News, officials in Virginia and Washington, D.C. turned to the courts to try to stop the project before significant work begins. The report indicates that the lawsuits are intended to secure a legal ruling on whether the planned arch complies with applicable rules governing major structures in the region.

CBS News reported that the monument is connected to Donald Trump, which has added political attention to what is formally a dispute over land use, monument approval, and jurisdiction. The suits, as described by the outlet, focus on the power of state and local governments to block or limit such a project.

The three pending lawsuits collectively underscore that more than one party is attempting to assert legal authority over the fate of the proposed arch, CBS News reported.

CBS News’ May 10 report establishes several core facts: a monument linked to Trump has been proposed; it is described as an arch about 250 feet tall that would rise over Washington, D.C.; and Virginia and the District of Columbia have filed lawsuits seeking to halt any work on it.
