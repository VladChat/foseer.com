The New York Giants have acquired quarterback JJ McCarthy from the Minnesota Vikings in exchange for a 2027 fifth-round draft pick, the Vikings announced Monday. The deal is pending McCarthy passing a physical.

The trade comes after Giants quarterback Jaxson Dart was ruled out for the season, according to reporting from the Guardian, leaving New York to move quickly for an experienced replacement.

## What the trade involves

According to the Vikings’ announcement, Minnesota will receive a 2027 fifth-round pick from the Giants in return for McCarthy. The Guardian reports that McCarthy had been a first-round selection for the Vikings in the 2024 NFL draft, underscoring the relatively modest price New York is paying two years later.

The agreement is not yet official on the league transaction wire and remains contingent on McCarthy completing and passing a physical examination. That condition is standard for NFL trades but means the move is not fully finalized.

The Guardian’s account places the announcement on Monday, with Minnesota disclosing the terms and the physical requirement. No additional conditions, such as pick swaps or performance escalators, have been publicly reported at this stage.

## Why the Giants moved now

The Guardian links the trade directly to the loss of Jaxson Dart, who has been ruled out for the remainder of the season. With their quarterback depth chart suddenly thinned, the Giants turned to Minnesota for a player who has recently been evaluated as a starting-caliber prospect.

By acquiring McCarthy, New York adds a passer who was valued highly enough to be taken in the first round in 2024. That status, reported by the Guardian, suggests the Giants are not simply filling a roster spot but attempting to stabilize the most important position on the field after Dart’s injury.

The Guardian’s reporting does not specify the exact nature of Dart’s injury, how it occurred, or when the team formally placed him on season-ending status. It does, however, make clear that his unavailability for the rest of the year is the backdrop for the Giants’ decision to trade future draft capital for immediate help.

## What it means for Minnesota

From Minnesota’s perspective, the trade moves on from a recent first-round investment in exchange for a mid-round pick three years down the line. The Guardian notes that McCarthy was selected in the first round of the 2024 draft, indicating that the Vikings once viewed him as a potential long-term answer at quarterback.

By accepting a 2027 fifth-rounder, the Vikings are opting for future flexibility over holding onto McCarthy. The Guardian’s report does not detail Minnesota’s internal reasoning, depth chart plans, or any prior indications that McCarthy might be available in trade discussions.

The move nonetheless marks a clear shift: a quarterback drafted in the first round just two years ago is being converted into a single mid-round pick, suggesting the Vikings are comfortable with their current direction at the position. Without additional corroborating reports, it is not yet possible to say more about how Minnesota intends to use the pick or reallocate McCarthy’s role.

## What’s at stake for both teams

For the Giants, the trade is a response to immediate need. The Guardian frames the move as directly tied to Dart’s season-ending status, with New York acting to secure a quarterback who has already been evaluated at a high level by another franchise.

The cost—a 2027 fifth-round pick—places a tangible but limited price on that urgency. Fifth-round picks often produce depth players or occasional starters, but they are not typically considered premium assets. By sending that selection to Minnesota, New York is effectively betting that stabilizing the quarterback position now is worth more than the chance to draft a developmental player three years from today.

For the Vikings, the stakes are different. Trading a former first-round pick for a single mid-round selection in a future draft represents a notable change from their 2024 plans. The Guardian’s reporting confirms the basic terms but does not provide detail on whether Minnesota had alternative offers or how widely they shopped McCarthy.

What is clear from the Guardian’s account is that the Vikings were willing to move on and that the Giants were motivated buyers in the wake of Dart’s injury. How the trade will be judged will depend on McCarthy’s performance in New York and what Minnesota ultimately does with the 2027 pick.

## What to watch next

The next formal step is McCarthy’s physical. As the Vikings’ announcement, relayed by the Guardian, makes clear, the trade is contingent on him passing. Only then will the deal be completed and reflected in official league records.

Once finalized, attention will turn to how quickly the Giants integrate McCarthy into their offense and how they structure the depth chart without Dart. On the other side, the Vikings will move forward with their remaining quarterbacks and an additional future draft asset.

The trade links two franchises at a moment of transition: New York reacting to a season-altering injury, and Minnesota reshaping its plans for a former first-round quarterback. With the basic terms now public, the impact will unfold on the field and in future draft rooms over the seasons ahead.
