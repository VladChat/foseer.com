Tony Romo, the former Dallas Cowboys quarterback who became the face of CBS’s NFL coverage, is not attending CBS’s NFL Media Day today, according to a report from the New York Times. The outlet reports that Romo “will not be at CBS's NFL Media Day today,” marking a conspicuous absence from an event typically used to showcase the network’s top football talent and storylines ahead of the coming season.

The same New York Times report notes that CBS Sports president David Berson has elevated the network’s No. 1 NFL broadcast team, underscoring that CBS is actively reshaping how it presents pro football to viewers. The details of that elevation, and how Romo fits into it, have not been fully described in the publicly available reporting.

While NFL, Congress, and FIFA are all prominent institutions in the broader sports and policy landscape, the current reporting from the New York Times focuses specifically on Romo’s absence from CBS’s NFL Media Day and Berson’s moves within CBS Sports. No direct connection between Romo’s absence and those institutions is established in the evidence available so far.

## What CBS’s NFL Media Day Usually Signals

NFL Media Day events at major networks are designed to introduce or reintroduce broadcast teams, outline coverage plans, and give reporters and partners a chance to hear directly from on-air talent and executives. For a lead analyst like Romo, who has been central to CBS’s NFL identity since joining the network after his playing career, presence at such a gathering would typically be expected.

By reporting that “Tony Romo will not be at CBS's NFL Media Day today,” the New York Times is highlighting a break from that expectation. The paper does not, in the material currently available, specify whether Romo’s absence is due to scheduling, personal reasons, or a strategic choice by the network, and there is no independently confirmed explanation yet in other outlets.

## David Berson’s Role in the Shift

The New York Times report adds that CBS Sports president David Berson has elevated the network’s top NFL broadcast team. In practice, such an elevation often involves giving that team more marquee games, greater promotional focus, or a clearer role as the signature voice of the network’s coverage.

The report does not spell out the precise nature of the elevation, but it places Berson’s decision and Romo’s absence in the same frame of reference: CBS is making decisions about how to present its NFL product, and its most recognizable analyst is not on hand at a key promotional event.

Without further corroborating detail, it is not yet clear whether Romo’s absence is directly connected to Berson’s elevation of the No. 1 team, or whether the timing is coincidental. The New York Times describes both developments but does not explicitly link them as cause and effect in the material currently available.

## Why Romo’s Absence Draws Attention

Romo’s move from the Dallas Cowboys to CBS several years ago turned him into one of the most discussed figures in football broadcasting. His early reputation for predicting plays and his high-profile contract with CBS made him a central part of the network’s NFL identity.

Against that backdrop, his absence from CBS’s NFL Media Day stands out. Media days are where networks reaffirm who their lead voices are and how they plan to cover the season. When a central figure is missing, it naturally raises questions about how prominently that person will feature in the months ahead.

At this stage, the only firmly supported facts are that Romo is not at the event today and that Berson has elevated CBS’s No. 1 NFL team, as reported by the New York Times. Any further interpretation about Romo’s long-term role would go beyond what the current evidence supports.

## What’s at Stake for Viewers and the Network

For viewers, broadcast teams shape how games feel: which storylines are emphasized, how rules and strategy are explained, and how big moments are framed. Romo’s presence in the booth has been a defining part of CBS’s coverage, especially for high-profile NFL matchups.

From the network’s perspective, the configuration of its top team—and how that team is promoted at events like NFL Media Day—can influence audience expectations and relationships with the league. The New York Times’ reporting that Berson has elevated the No. 1 team suggests CBS is refining that configuration, though the specifics remain limited in the public record.

## What to Watch Next

The key confirmed development is narrow but notable: Romo is not at CBS’s NFL Media Day today, and CBS Sports president David Berson has moved to elevate the network’s top NFL team, according to the New York Times.

Further clarity will likely depend on additional reporting from CBS, Romo, or other outlets explaining whether his absence is a one-day scheduling matter or part of a broader adjustment in the network’s NFL plans. Until more independently verified information emerges, the main takeaway is that CBS’s most prominent football analyst is missing from a major preseason stage at a moment when its sports leadership is actively reshaping the network’s top NFL broadcast presence.
