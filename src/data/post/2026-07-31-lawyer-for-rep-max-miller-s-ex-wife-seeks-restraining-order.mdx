The attorney for the ex-wife of Republican Rep. Max Miller of Ohio has asked a court to issue a restraining order that would bar the congressman from contacting her lawyer, according to reporting by CBS News on May 10.

CBS News reports that the filing comes as Miller faces allegations of abuse from his former spouse, allegations the congressman has denied. The request asks the court to limit Miller’s direct contact with the attorney representing his ex-wife while those claims are being addressed.

## What the court filing seeks

According to CBS News, the lawyer for Miller’s ex-wife petitioned the court for a restraining order aimed specifically at preventing Miller from contacting her legal counsel. The report indicates the request focuses on communication boundaries rather than broader limits on Miller’s movements.

CBS News describes the filing as part of an ongoing dispute connected to abuse allegations raised by Miller’s former wife. The report does not detail the full text of the petition or the precise terms requested, but characterizes it as an effort to restrict Miller from reaching out directly to her attorney while the matter is before the court.

CBS News also reports that Miller has denied the abuse allegations. The story notes that his denial is part of the public record surrounding the case, though additional independent corroboration of the underlying claims remains limited at this stage.

## Who is involved

The case centers on Rep. Max Miller, a Republican member of the U.S. House from Ohio, and his ex-wife, who brought the abuse allegations described in the CBS News report. Her name and identifying details are not fully laid out in the summary of the filing reported by CBS News.

The restraining order request was filed by the ex-wife’s attorney, whose role is to represent her interests in the legal dispute. CBS News identifies that lawyer as the party formally asking the court to act, rather than the ex-wife filing the petition directly.

No other individuals or institutions are identified in the CBS News account as parties to the restraining order request, beyond the court that will decide whether to grant or deny it.

## How this could affect the dispute

CBS News reports that the restraining order request is tied to the broader conflict between Miller and his ex-wife over the abuse allegations. If granted, the order would formalize limits on how the congressman can communicate with her attorney while the case continues.

Such an order, as described by CBS News, would not resolve the abuse claims themselves. Instead, it would shape the conditions under which the parties and their representatives interact as those claims are considered. The report indicates this is an early-stage procedural step rather than a final determination about the allegations.

CBS News notes that Miller denies the abuse accusations. The outlet also indicates that independent corroboration of the claims is limited so far and that further reporting will be needed as additional court records or statements become available.
