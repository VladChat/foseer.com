A new British drama titled *Faith* is drawing attention at the Toronto International Film Festival for its tightly controlled tension and two central performances that critics describe as intense and unsettling. In an early review from the Guardian, the film is characterised as a “startling and involving” mystery built around a mysterious suitor whose motives remain unclear.

The film is written and directed by British filmmaker Paul Andrew Williams and stars Erin Doherty and James McAvoy. The Guardian’s account places the story in a downbeat town and centres on a single mother whose quiet, self-contained life is disrupted when an enigmatic stranger begins to pursue her.

## A tense mystery built around a single mother

According to the Guardian review, *Faith* follows a happily single mother, played by Erin Doherty, who lives in a bleak, economically depressed town. Her life is described as settled and intentionally solitary until she is approached by a man whose interest in her quickly becomes the film’s central source of tension.

The review notes that the drama is structured as an eerie mystery, with the narrative withholding clear answers about who this man is and what he wants. This uncertainty is presented as deliberate, keeping the audience aligned with Doherty’s character as she tries to assess whether the attention she is receiving is romantic, threatening, or something more complicated.

The Guardian’s critic highlights that much of the film’s impact comes from how it sustains unease rather than overtly explaining events, describing the overall effect as “startling and unsettling.”

## Erin Doherty and James McAvoy at the film’s centre

The Guardian review states that both Erin Doherty and James McAvoy “bring their A‑game” to the film. Doherty, best known to many viewers for her television work, is described as carrying the story as a woman who appears content in her independence but is gradually pushed into a more precarious emotional state.

McAvoy plays the mysterious suitor whose presence drives the plot. As reported by the Guardian, his performance is key to the film’s atmosphere: the character’s charm, opacity and potential menace are kept in careful balance, so that viewers, like the protagonist, are never entirely sure how to interpret him.

The review emphasises that the film relies heavily on the interplay between these two performances rather than on large-scale set pieces or elaborate plotting. Their scenes together are described as intense and central to the film’s unsettling tone.

## Paul Andrew Williams’s restrained, taut direction

The Guardian identifies *Faith* as the latest feature from writer-director Paul Andrew Williams and notes his ongoing productivity and creativity as a positive sign for the British film industry. Within this review, his work on *Faith* is described as “expertly taut,” suggesting a tight focus on mood, pacing and character rather than a sprawling narrative.

According to the Guardian’s account, Williams uses the downbeat town setting to reinforce the story’s sense of isolation and unease. The film is portrayed as grounded in everyday detail, with the strangeness arising from the suitor’s behaviour and the protagonist’s growing uncertainty rather than from overtly supernatural or sensational elements.

The review credits Williams with maintaining a controlled tension throughout, allowing the mystery to unfold gradually. This approach, as described, keeps the audience engaged in the central question of who this man is and what his presence will ultimately mean for the single mother at the film’s centre.

## Early critical reception and what is known so far

At this stage, detailed information about *Faith* beyond the Guardian’s coverage remains limited. The Guardian review is among the first substantial accounts of the film’s plot, tone and performances to appear following its screening at the Toronto International Film Festival.

Within that review, the film is portrayed as a contained, character-driven drama that leans into psychological tension rather than action. The critic’s emphasis on the “startling and unsettling” nature of the story, and on the strength of Doherty and McAvoy’s performances, positions *Faith* as a notable entry in the festival’s lineup.

The Guardian also frames Williams’s continued output as “an industry tonic,” suggesting that his ability to produce new work at a steady pace is seen as encouraging within the British film landscape. However, broader industry reactions, audience responses, and additional critical perspectives have not yet been widely reported.

## Why this film’s debut matters

Based on the Guardian’s reporting, *Faith* stands out at Toronto as an example of a low-key but carefully constructed mystery that depends on performance and atmosphere rather than spectacle. For viewers and industry observers, the film’s reception may help shape perceptions of Erin Doherty’s trajectory as a leading actor and reaffirm James McAvoy’s reputation for complex, ambiguous roles.

The Guardian’s description of Paul Andrew Williams’s work on *Faith* also underscores his ongoing role in British independent cinema. As more reviews and audience reactions emerge, they will clarify whether the early praise for the film’s intensity and unsettling tone translates into wider attention beyond the festival circuit.
