The United States’ historic win over Paraguay will be remembered for the result. But what made it feel genuinely new was how it looked and felt: loose, expressive, and, as Mauricio Pochettino put it, “winning a lot of fans” with a style built on fun as much as fight, according to reporting in the Guardian.

For a program long associated with grit and grind, this performance suggested a different identity taking shape. The question now is what concrete step comes next to lock that identity in.

## A historic result, and a different kind of US team

The Guardian’s match report frames the Paraguay victory as a result that required a trip deep into the archives to find a precedent, reaching back to early World Cup eras when teams crossed the ocean by ship and played with heavy leather balls.

That comparison underlines two ideas at once: the rarity of the achievement and the sense that this US performance did not fit the old stereotype of the national team. Instead of a purely workmanlike side, the article describes a team playing with freedom, confidence and visible enjoyment.

Pochettino’s observation that the US are “winning a lot of fans” captures how the manner of the win matters as much as the margin. It suggests that, in this match at least, the US were not just surviving; they were entertaining.

## From grit-and-grind to something more expansive

For years, the US men’s national team has often been summed up with the same set of adjectives: organized, hard‑working, physically committed. The Guardian piece explicitly contrasts that image with what unfolded against Paraguay, noting that the side is “no longer a workmanlike outfit built on grit and grind.”

That contrast is not just about style points. A team that relies solely on defensive solidity and effort tends to be described as difficult to beat rather than exciting to watch. By emphasizing the “fun and free nature” of this win, the reporting points to a tactical and psychological shift:

- **Tactically**, “fun and free” implies more risk‑taking on the ball, more willingness to play through pressure, and more players trusted to make creative decisions rather than simply follow rigid patterns.
- **Psychologically**, it suggests a group playing without the tightness that can come with fear of mistakes, particularly in high‑stakes games.

The Paraguay match, as described, becomes a kind of proof‑of‑concept for that shift: the US can play with freedom and still secure a result significant enough to be called historic.

## Why ‘fun’ is not just a feel‑good label

Calling a performance “fun” can sound superficial, but in this case it points to something more structural. The Guardian’s emphasis on enjoyment and freedom signals that the players were not only executing a plan but also expressing themselves within it.

In elite football, that matters in three concrete ways:

1. **Player confidence** – A team that feels allowed to play with freedom is more likely to attempt the risky passes or runs that unlock defenses. The reporting’s focus on how liberated this US side looked suggests that confidence was a visible part of the performance.
2. **Public perception** – Pochettino’s comment that the US are “winning a lot of fans” ties directly to how the match looked from the outside. A team that plays with joy is easier to support, especially for neutral viewers deciding whether to invest emotionally in a side.
3. **Identity building** – For a national team, style of play becomes part of its identity. The Guardian’s framing of this match as a break from the “grit and grind” past hints that this could be a reference point for how the US wants to be seen going forward.

In that sense, “fun” is less about smiles and celebrations and more about a deliberate choice to lean into a more expressive, attacking identity.

## Who gains from this shift in style?

The immediate winners from a performance like this are straightforward: the players, the coaching staff, and a fan base that has long wanted the US to compete with more than just effort.

Based on the Guardian’s account, several groups stand to benefit if this style is sustained:

- **Players** gain proof that they can play expansively on a big stage and still deliver a historic result. That can change how they approach future high‑pressure matches.
- **Coaches and decision‑makers** gain validation for trusting a more open game model, rather than defaulting to conservative tactics in important fixtures.
- **Supporters** gain a team that is not only successful but also enjoyable to watch, which aligns with Pochettino’s observation about winning fans.

There is a broader, implied stake as well: the reputation of US soccer. While the Guardian piece focuses on this specific match, its language suggests that performances like this can shift how the program is perceived abroad—from plucky and stubborn to technically capable and entertaining.

## What to watch for after a win like this

Within the narrow evidence available, the Paraguay match stands as a snapshot: a United States team playing with freedom, enjoying itself, and earning a result significant enough to draw comparisons with early World Cup history.

The key follow‑up questions that future reporting can answer are straightforward:

- Do the US maintain this “fun and free” style in their next major fixtures?
- Do coaches and players publicly claim this approach as their long‑term identity?
- Does the sense of the team “winning a lot of fans,” as Pochettino put it, show up in how crowds, broadcasters and international observers talk about them?

Those are the concrete developments most likely to be confirmed next. For now, the Paraguay win stands as a clear marker: a day when the United States did not just win, but did so in a way that felt different—and, crucially, enjoyable—for those on the field and those watching from afar.
