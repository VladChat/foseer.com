Rachel Fleit’s documentary *The Slightest Touch* follows actor Colin Farrell and Irish advocate Emma Fogarty as they prepare to run the Dublin Marathon together to raise awareness of epidermolysis bullosa, a rare and painful skin condition. The film, reviewed by the Guardian, is described as both carefully controlled in what it shows and unexpectedly intimate in how it captures their shared effort.

According to the Guardian’s account, Farrell and Fogarty first met in 2010 and have maintained a connection that eventually led to this marathon project. The documentary focuses on their preparation for the Dublin race and the emotional weight of linking a high-profile celebrity to a largely unfamiliar medical condition.

## A controlled but personal portrait

The Guardian’s review notes that the personal scenes in *The Slightest Touch* are tightly curated. Fleit appears to have limited how much of Farrell’s and Fogarty’s private lives enters the frame, with the paper emphasizing that privacy is important to both of them for different reasons.

Within those boundaries, the film is still described as “unexpectedly intimate” and emotional. The review suggests that this intimacy comes less from revealing private details and more from watching the pair commit to a physically demanding goal together, with the marathon serving as a visible test of endurance and trust.

## Running for epidermolysis bullosa awareness

Central to the documentary is Fogarty’s experience with epidermolysis bullosa, often shortened to EB. The Guardian explains that Farrell and Fogarty’s marathon effort is explicitly framed as a way to raise awareness of the condition.

While the review does not dwell on medical detail, it makes clear that the film links the physical strain of marathon training with the daily challenges associated with EB. By following the pair through their preparation for the Dublin race, Fleit’s documentary positions the event as both a personal milestone and a public platform.

## Farrell and Fogarty’s long-running connection

The Guardian reports that Farrell and Fogarty first met in 2010. Over the years, that relationship developed into a partnership focused on advocacy, culminating in the decision to run the Dublin Marathon together.

In the review’s telling, their shared history underpins the emotional tone of the film. Rather than presenting the marathon as a one-off gesture, the documentary situates it within a longer relationship in which Farrell uses his public profile to support Fogarty’s cause, while Fogarty anchors the story in lived experience.

## Director Rachel Fleit’s approach

Fleit, whose previous work has included character-driven documentaries, is described by the Guardian as “considerate” in how she shapes *The Slightest Touch*. The review highlights her decision to respect the boundaries set by both subjects while still crafting a film that feels emotionally open.

That balance appears central to the documentary’s impact as described in the review: the film avoids intrusive exposure yet still invites viewers into the physical and emotional demands of training and advocacy. The Guardian’s assessment suggests that this approach results in a “big” emotional payoff, even though the scenes themselves are selective and controlled.

## Why this documentary matters

Based on the Guardian’s review, *The Slightest Touch* stands out less as a conventional sports story and more as a focused portrait of how a public figure and a patient advocate share a demanding project to draw attention to a little-known condition. The film uses the structure of marathon training and the Dublin race to make that effort visible.

As additional reviews and audience reactions emerge, they will help clarify how widely the documentary resonates and how effectively it advances awareness of epidermolysis bullosa beyond the immediate circle of Farrell, Fogarty and their existing supporters.
