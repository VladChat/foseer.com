The New York Knicks moved within two wins of their first NBA championship in more than half a century on Friday night, holding off the San Antonio Spurs 105-104 in a tense Game 2 to take a 2-0 lead in the NBA Finals, according to reporting from the Guardian.

The Guardian’s game account describes a tight contest in San Antonio in which Jalen Brunson again drove New York’s offense, helping the Knicks complete a sweep of the opening two games on the road. The victory sends the series back to Madison Square Garden with New York holding what is widely considered a commanding advantage.

## Brunson leads Knicks to second straight road win

Across coverage from the Guardian, Jalen Brunson is repeatedly identified as the central figure in New York’s run through these Finals. In Friday’s Game 2, he again led the Knicks’ attack, steering them through what the outlet characterizes as a physical, low-margin battle.

The Guardian’s reports frame the game as another example of Brunson’s ability to control tempo and create offense under pressure. While specific play-by-play details are not fully laid out in the available coverage, Brunson is consistently cited as the engine of New York’s scoring and late-game composure.

New York’s 105-104 win, as reported by the Guardian, did not come easily. San Antonio pushed the game to the final possessions, forcing the Knicks to execute in half-court sets and withstand defensive pressure. Brunson’s decision-making and shot creation are singled out in the coverage as decisive factors in New York’s ability to close out the contest.

## A 2-0 series lead heading back to New York

With the Game 2 win, the Knicks now lead the best-of-seven series 2-0. The Guardian notes that New York will carry that edge back to Madison Square Garden, where the next two games are scheduled.

In NBA Finals history, a 2-0 lead has typically placed the team in front in a strong position, especially when it has also taken both games on the road. The Guardian’s reporting underscores the significance of New York’s sweep in San Antonio: the Knicks have not only defended their own momentum from Game 1 but also taken full control of home-court advantage.

The series format gives New York the chance to play the next two games in front of its home crowd. While the Guardian’s pieces do not project outcomes beyond the current games, the implication is clear: San Antonio now faces the task of winning at least one game at Madison Square Garden simply to extend the series.

## Spurs face uphill battle after home-court sweep

For the Spurs, the narrow defeat deepens the challenge. The Guardian’s coverage emphasizes that San Antonio has now dropped both Finals games on its own floor, a scenario that leaves little margin for error.

Losing 105-104 in Game 2, after already falling in Game 1, means the Spurs must win four of the next five games to claim the championship, with at least two of those wins needing to come on the road in New York. While the Guardian does not quantify the odds, its description of the situation makes clear that San Antonio is now in what it calls an “uphill battle” to get back into the series.

The reporting highlights how thin the margin was in Game 2. A one-point loss suggests that small swings in execution, officiating decisions, or shot-making could have produced a different result. Nonetheless, the concrete outcome is that New York, not San Antonio, leaves Texas with both wins and the psychological edge that comes with them.

## Fifty-three years of waiting in New York

Context from a separate Guardian feature on this Finals run notes that the Knicks are chasing their first NBA title in more than 50 years. The outlet describes New York’s campaign as shaped by decades of anxiety and frustration among fans, pointing out that the franchise has not lifted the championship trophy since the early 1970s.

That long wait forms the backdrop to Friday’s win. The Guardian’s analysis portrays this Finals as a release valve for more than half a century of near-misses and rebuilding cycles. In that context, each victory—especially a tense, one-point road win like Game 2—carries emotional weight for the fan base and institutional significance for the franchise.

The repeated references in the Guardian’s coverage to “53 years of dread” underscore how closely this run is tied to the Knicks’ identity and history. Game 2 is not just another road victory; it is another step toward ending one of the longest active title droughts for a major NBA market.

## What’s at stake as the series shifts to the Garden

With the series moving to New York, the stakes for both teams are clear, as outlined in the Guardian’s reporting.

For the Knicks, two home games now stand between them and the brink of a championship that has eluded the franchise for generations. The Guardian’s accounts suggest that Brunson’s leadership and the team’s ability to navigate tightly contested games have become defining features of this run. Protecting home court in at least two of the remaining possible games at Madison Square Garden would secure the title.

For the Spurs, the task is immediate and unforgiving. After losing both games in San Antonio, they must find answers quickly. The Guardian’s game coverage indicates that San Antonio has been competitive—Game 2 was decided by a single point—but competitiveness alone will no longer be enough. The Spurs need wins, and they need them in one of the league’s most charged playoff environments.

As the Finals resume in New York, the central questions, grounded in the Guardian’s reporting, are straightforward: Can the Knicks, led by Brunson, convert a 2-0 lead and decades of pent-up ambition into a long-awaited championship? And can the Spurs recover from a home-court sweep to extend a series that now tilts heavily toward New York?

Those answers will begin to take shape at Madison Square Garden, with the Knicks now just two wins from ending a 53-year title drought and the Spurs fighting to keep their season alive.
