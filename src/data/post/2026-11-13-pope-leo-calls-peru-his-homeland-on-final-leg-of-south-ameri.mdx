## Why the “Homeland” Remark Matters

Papal language is often carefully chosen, and CBS News highlights this particular phrase because it suggests a relationship with Peru that goes beyond formal diplomacy or ceremonial obligation.

By calling Peru his “homeland,” the pope is not making a legal or constitutional claim about nationality. Instead, as described in the CBS News account, he appears to be speaking about a place that shaped his vocation and style of ministry. In Catholic practice, a “pastor” is a shepherd of a community, responsible for spiritual guidance and care. Saying that Peru taught him how to be a pastor suggests that experiences there influenced how he understands and exercises leadership.
