Baltimore Ravens cornerback Marlon Humphrey was deactivated for the team’s road game against the Carolina Panthers on Sunday after he said Baltimore excels at “making criminals,” a remark that drew public criticism from the city’s mayor, according to reporting by the Guardian.

The 30-year-old veteran was ruled out of the game following the release of a recent episode of his podcast in which he made the comment about the city. Humphrey later apologized, but Baltimore’s mayor said the apology was not sufficient, the Guardian reported.

## Humphrey’s comment and team response

Humphrey made the remark on the latest episode of his podcast, describing Baltimore as a place that excels at “making criminals,” according to the Guardian’s account of the recording. The comment quickly drew attention because Humphrey plays for the city’s NFL team and has been a prominent figure in the community.

The Ravens responded by deactivating Humphrey for Sunday’s game at Carolina. The Guardian reported that the decision meant Humphrey, a starting-caliber cornerback, was unavailable as the team prepared to face the Panthers.

The franchise did not immediately provide a detailed public explanation tying the benching directly to the podcast remark, but the Guardian’s reporting linked the timing of the deactivation to the fallout from Humphrey’s comment.

## Backlash in Baltimore and mayor’s criticism

Humphrey’s description of Baltimore prompted criticism from local officials. The Guardian reported that the city’s mayor said Humphrey’s subsequent apology was “not enough,” signaling that local leaders viewed the comment as harmful to the city’s image and residents.

While the Guardian did not quote the mayor at length, its report stated that the mayor rejected the idea that a brief apology could fully address the impact of a high-profile player characterizing Baltimore as a city that “makes criminals.” The reaction underscored the sensitivity around public portrayals of Baltimore, which has long grappled with national narratives about crime and safety.

Humphrey issued an apology after the remark drew attention, according to the Guardian. Details of the apology, including its exact wording and where it was delivered, were not fully described in the outlet’s summary, but the report made clear that city leadership did not consider the matter closed.

## Locker room reaction and teammate’s view

Inside the Ravens’ locker room, at least one teammate publicly acknowledged Humphrey’s personality while discussing the controversy. The Guardian reported that a teammate described Humphrey as someone who can be “abrasive,” a characterization that suggested his outspoken style was familiar to those around him.

The same report indicated that, despite that description, the teammate did not dismiss Humphrey outright. Instead, the comment pointed to a player known for blunt or provocative remarks, which in this case spilled into a broader civic debate about how Baltimore is portrayed by its most visible figures.

The Guardian did not report any formal discipline from the NFL itself, and there was no indication from its coverage that the league office had intervened. The focus remained on the team’s decision to deactivate Humphrey and the local political response.

## What this means for the Ravens and Baltimore

Humphrey’s deactivation removed a veteran cornerback from the Ravens’ lineup for at least one game, affecting the team’s depth in the secondary against Carolina. The Guardian’s report did not specify whether the team framed the move as a one-game response or part of a longer-term disciplinary approach.

For Baltimore’s civic leaders, the episode highlighted ongoing tension over how the city is described by public figures. The mayor’s statement, as summarized by the Guardian, indicated concern that a star athlete’s comment about “making criminals” could reinforce negative stereotypes at a time when officials are working to improve the city’s reputation.

The key developments to watch, based on the Guardian’s reporting, are whether the Ravens clarify the length or terms of Humphrey’s deactivation and whether team officials or city leaders seek further dialogue with the player about his remarks. For now, the confirmed facts are that Humphrey made the comment on his podcast, apologized, was deactivated for the Panthers game, and continues to face criticism from Baltimore’s mayor and scrutiny from the public.
