Cameroon’s president, Paul Biya, has not been seen in public or heard from in more than seven weeks, a record stretch of silence that has intensified questions at home about his health and grip on power.

According to reporting by the *Guardian*, Biya left Cameroon on 7 June for what was officially described as a “brief private stay” at a hotel in Switzerland. Since then, he has not appeared in public or issued a statement, marking at least 53 days without a confirmed sighting or address.

The *Guardian* reports that this is the longest such absence of Biya’s presidency and notes that he is regarded as the world’s oldest serving head of state. The extended silence has reignited speculation inside Cameroon about whether the 91‑year‑old leader is alive or capable of governing, even as authorities move to restrict discussion of his health.

## A 53-day gap with no public word

Biya’s last known public activity was his departure for Switzerland on 7 June, the *Guardian* reports, with officials at the time characterising the trip as a short private stay. No images, videos, or speeches from the president have been released since.

The newspaper describes the current gap as a record absence for Biya, who has ruled Cameroon for decades. While the presidency has not announced any change in leadership, the lack of verifiable appearances has become the central fact driving public uncertainty.

The *Guardian* account does not provide independent visual or documentary confirmation of Biya’s condition beyond official statements about his June travel, and it notes that independent corroboration of his status remains limited. That scarcity of verifiable information has made the length of the silence itself a focal point of concern.

## Government clamps down on health talk

The *Guardian* reports that Cameroonian authorities have responded to the growing speculation by banning discussion of the president’s health. The outlet describes measures aimed at curbing public debate, though it does not detail the specific legal instruments or penalties involved.

By restricting conversation about Biya’s condition, officials appear to be trying to contain rumours and maintain an image of continuity at the top of the state. At the same time, the move has underscored how little verified information is available to citizens about the president’s whereabouts or wellbeing.

The *Guardian* frames this clampdown as part of a broader pattern in which the presidency closely guards details of Biya’s health and movements. In this case, however, the reported 53‑day absence has pushed that secrecy into sharper public view.

## A nonagenarian leader at the centre of uncertainty

Biya is described by the *Guardian* as the world’s oldest head of state, a status that shapes how Cameroonians interpret his prolonged silence. At 91, any extended disappearance from public life naturally raises health questions, particularly in a political system so closely identified with a single individual.

The newspaper reports that the combination of his age, the length of the absence since 7 June, and the formal ban on discussing his health has led to renewed speculation over whether he is dead or alive. These reactions are presented as public sentiment and rumour rather than confirmed fact.

The *Guardian* does not report any official medical bulletin, hospitalisation notice, or succession move from within Cameroon’s government. In the absence of such information, the article treats the duration of the silence, rather than any specific medical detail, as the central, verifiable development.

## Why the silence matters beyond the palace

The *Guardian* notes that Biya’s disappearance from view matters because of the authority he holds as head of state and the potential impact of any sudden change at the top. While the article does not lay out a detailed map of possible consequences, it points to concerns that uncertainty around his status could affect decision‑making and stability in Cameroon.

The report highlights that questions about whether the president is able to govern are not just personal or medical; they touch on how key state decisions are made and communicated when the leader is absent from public life for an extended period.

## Limited confirmation, growing questions

The *Guardian* explicitly acknowledges that independent corroboration of Biya’s current condition is limited. Beyond the official description of his 7 June departure for a brief private stay in Switzerland, the paper does not cite additional on‑the‑record sources confirming his whereabouts or health.

That lack of clear, verifiable information is central to the story. It has allowed rumours to spread while leaving citizens and observers reliant on a small number of official statements and media reports. The *Guardian* presents the 53‑day absence, the ban on discussing his health, and Biya’s status as the world’s oldest head of state as the key confirmed elements of a situation still marked by uncertainty.

## What to watch next

For now, the most concrete facts are Biya’s departure from Cameroon on 7 June for a private stay in Switzerland, his reported record 53‑day stretch without public appearance or speech, and the government’s move to restrict discussion of his health, as reported by the *Guardian*.

Further developments are likely to hinge on whether Cameroonian authorities release new, verifiable information about the president’s condition or schedule, and whether any formal steps are taken to clarify who is exercising executive authority during his prolonged silence.

Until then, the unanswered questions around the world’s oldest head of state remain a source of unease in a country accustomed to seeing its political life organised around a single, long‑serving figure.
