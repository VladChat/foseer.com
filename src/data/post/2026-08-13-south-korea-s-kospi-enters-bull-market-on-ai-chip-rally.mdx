South Korea’s main stock index, the Kospi, has entered a technical bull market after a sharp rally in chipmakers tied to artificial intelligence (AI), according to reporting from CNBC. The move, which took place during Thursday’s session in Asia, was driven by gains in heavyweight semiconductor names including SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics, with sentiment also linked to strength in U.S. AI leader Nvidia.

A market is typically described as being in a “technical bull market” when a major index rises 20% or more from its most recent significant low. CNBC reported that the Kospi crossed that threshold on Thursday, underscoring how enthusiasm for AI-related hardware is reshaping South Korean equity trading.

## What Pushed the Kospi Into a Bull Market

CNBC’s event-focused coverage of Thursday’s trading session attributed the Kospi’s latest rise largely to chipmakers that are central to the AI supply chain.

SK Hynix, one of the world’s largest memory chip producers, was highlighted as a key driver. The company manufactures high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, a type of advanced memory used in AI accelerators. These accelerators are specialized chips that speed up AI computations in data centers.

Samsung Electronics, another heavyweight in the index and a major global memory and logic chip producer, also advanced in Thursday’s trade, adding to the index’s upward momentum. Because both SK Hynix and Samsung carry significant weight in the Kospi, moves in their share prices can have an outsized impact on the overall index level.

The rally unfolded over the course of Thursday’s trading hours, with CNBC noting that South Korean stocks broadly moved higher and that the Kospi’s gain was sufficient to meet the standard 20% threshold that defines a technical bull market.

## The AI Trade and Nvidia’s Role

The latest gains in South Korean chipmakers came against the backdrop of strong global interest in AI-related stocks. A separate CNBC analysis of the “AI trade” pointed to Nvidia’s continued strength as an important factor supporting investor confidence in companies that supply or benefit from AI infrastructure.

Nvidia designs graphics processing units (GPUs) that are widely used to train and run large AI models. CNBC’s contextual reporting described how demand for these chips has supported a broader ecosystem of suppliers and partners. Within that ecosystem, SK Hynix and Samsung are important memory suppliers to AI chip platforms.

That relationship has helped frame South Korean chipmakers as indirect beneficiaries of Nvidia’s growth. As investors look for ways to participate in the AI build-out beyond U.S.-listed stocks, they have increasingly focused on companies that provide critical components, such as high-performance memory, to AI data centers. CNBC’s coverage linked this broader AI enthusiasm to the buying interest seen in South Korean semiconductor names.

## How the Rally Shows Up in Trading

According to CNBC’s reporting, the Kospi’s move into bull-market territory was not the result of a single intraday spike but of cumulative gains that have built over recent weeks and months, culminating in Thursday’s advance.

The trading session highlighted several key features:

- **Concentration in chipmakers:** SK Hynix and Samsung, as large-cap semiconductor stocks, were among the most closely watched names in the day’s trade. Their gains provided much of the index’s lift.
- **Index-level impact:** Because the Kospi is weighted by market capitalization, large technology and chip companies can drive index performance even if other sectors move less.
- **AI-linked narrative:** CNBC’s reports repeatedly referenced the AI trade in describing investor behavior, indicating that market participants were specifically buying into companies perceived as AI beneficiaries rather than simply rotating into equities in general.

While the precise intraday percentage moves for each stock were not detailed in the available reporting, CNBC’s characterization of the session emphasized that the semiconductor-led advance was strong enough to push the Kospi over the 20% threshold from its prior low.

## Why the Kospi’s Bull Market Label Matters

The designation of a technical bull market is a market convention rather than a legal or regulatory status, but it can still influence how investors interpret conditions.

By noting that the Kospi has risen at least 20% from its recent low, CNBC’s coverage signaled that South Korean equities—especially chipmakers—have moved decisively away from their previous trough. For some investors, this can serve as a psychological marker that a downtrend has ended and a new upward phase has begun, even though it does not guarantee future gains.

The fact that AI-related chip stocks are at the center of this shift underscores how a single theme can shape an entire index. With SK Hynix and Samsung among the largest components of the Kospi, strong demand for AI hardware has translated into index-level strength.

At the same time, CNBC’s contextual reporting on the AI trade, including the role of Nvidia and specialized cloud providers such as CoreWeave, shows that the story is not limited to one country’s market. Instead, it is part of a global revaluation of companies tied to AI infrastructure. Within that global picture, South Korea’s Kospi has now registered a formal, technical bull-market move.

## What to Watch Next

CNBC’s reports did not provide detailed forecasts for South Korean stocks, but they did frame Thursday’s session as part of a broader AI-driven rally in chipmakers. For readers following the Kospi, several elements will likely remain central:

- Ongoing demand for AI chips and related hardware
- Trading performance of SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics
- Developments affecting Nvidia and other core AI hardware providers that shape sentiment toward suppliers

For now, the confirmed development is clear: as of Thursday’s trading in Asia, South Korea’s Kospi index has climbed into a technical bull market, propelled by renewed enthusiasm for AI-related semiconductor stocks.
