The Milwaukee Brewers opened the World Series with a tense 3-2 win over the Tampa Bay Rays, surviving a chaotic replay review and a ninth-inning rally before closer Trevor Megill struck out Jonny DeLuca with the tying and go‑ahead runs in scoring position, according to game reporting from the Guardian.

Milwaukee’s early offense, led by Christian Yelich in the first inning, and late pitching from rookie Robert Gasser Misiorowski and Megill proved just enough to withstand Tampa Bay’s late push in Game 1.

## Early jolt from Yelich sets Brewers’ tone

The Guardian’s account of the opener describes Milwaukee jumping on the Rays immediately. In the first inning, Christian Yelich drove a triple into the gap to bring home the game’s opening run, then came around to score himself, staking the Brewers to an early advantage.

That two-run surge in the first proved critical in a game that settled into a low-scoring duel. With both teams’ pitching staffs largely in control after the opening frame, every early baserunner carried extra weight. The Guardian report notes that Yelich’s swing and subsequent run gave Milwaukee the cushion it would lean on all night.

## Pitching duel keeps score tight

From there, the World Series opener evolved into a tight contest centered on run prevention. The Guardian’s game story highlights how Brewers pitching limited Tampa Bay’s opportunities through the middle innings, while the Rays’ staff kept Milwaukee from breaking the game open.

Rookie right-hander Jacob Misiorowski (identified in the Guardian coverage as part of the Brewers’ late-game effort) and reliever Trevor Megill were particularly important in the closing stages. Misiorowski helped bridge the gap from the starters to the ninth, holding off a Rays lineup that began to threaten more consistently as the game wore on.

Tampa Bay, meanwhile, chipped away at the deficit rather than mounting one big inning. By the late frames, the Rays had narrowed Milwaukee’s lead to a single run, setting the stage for the tense finish.

## Chaotic replay drama in the ninth inning

The most contentious moment came in the ninth inning, when a close play triggered a replay review that briefly threw the game into confusion. The Guardian report characterizes the sequence as “chaotic,” with the outcome of the review carrying major implications for the Rays’ comeback bid and the Brewers’ attempt to close out the win.

While specific camera angles and internal league deliberations are not detailed in the available reporting, the Guardian’s description makes clear that the review centered on a pivotal play involving baserunners in the final inning. The call on the field, and its confirmation or overturn on replay, directly affected how many Rays remained on base and how many outs Milwaukee still needed.

Because current independent corroboration of the exact mechanics of the replay sequence is limited, the Guardian’s account is the primary basis for understanding this moment. What is clear from that reporting is that the stoppage extended the inning, heightened the pressure on both teams, and left the Brewers needing one more critical pitch to secure the win.

## Megill slams the door with tying runs aboard

After the review, the Rays still had a chance to steal Game 1. The Guardian’s coverage notes that Tampa Bay managed to put runners on second and third in the ninth, turning the final at‑bat into a high‑leverage showdown.

Trevor Megill, tasked with closing the game for Milwaukee, faced Jonny DeLuca with two outs and the Brewers clinging to their 3-2 lead. With the tying run on second and the potential go‑ahead run on third, any ball in the gap or down the line could have flipped the result.

According to the Guardian’s game report, Megill responded by striking out DeLuca, ending the Rays’ rally and sealing Milwaukee’s 3-2 victory. That strikeout capped a tense inning that had already featured the controversial replay and multiple Rays baserunners.

## What this Game 1 result means

Within the scope of the available reporting, the implications are straightforward: Milwaukee takes a 1-0 lead in the best‑of‑seven World Series, while Tampa Bay is left to regroup after coming one hit away from a comeback in the opener.

The Guardian account underlines two themes from Game 1. First, the Brewers’ ability to score early and then rely on their pitching staff, including Misiorowski and Megill, gave them a path to win even as the offense cooled. Second, the late replay drama underscores how fine the margins can be in a championship series, where a single reviewed play can reshape an inning and, potentially, the series narrative.

Because independent verification beyond the Guardian’s event‑direct report is limited at this stage, details of the replay sequence and some aspects of the late‑inning strategy should be treated as provisional and monitored as additional game accounts and official explanations are published.

For now, the essential facts are clear from the Guardian’s coverage: Milwaukee rode Yelich’s first‑inning burst, weathered Tampa Bay’s late charge, navigated a confusing replay review, and ultimately relied on Megill’s final strikeout of DeLuca with runners on second and third to secure a 3-2 win and an early edge in the World Series.
