The Golden State Valkyries, the WNBA’s newest franchise backed by the NBA’s Golden State Warriors ownership group, have made league history with the speed and scale of their launch, according to reporting by the New York Times on May 10. The team, led by owner Joe Lacob and head coach Natalie Nakase, moved from formal approval to a fully branded, heavily marketed, and locally visible operation in a time frame that veteran league observers described as unusually fast for a new professional sports team.

The Times report describes Lacob and Nakase celebrating a key early milestone one day before publication, clasping hands as the organization marked how quickly the Valkyries had shifted from an expansion idea to an on-the-ground reality in the Bay Area. That rapid rollout, the report indicates, is what has set the Valkyries apart and underpins claims that the franchise has already made history.

## A franchise built at uncommon speed

According to the New York Times account, the Valkyries’ path from league approval to operational readiness has been compressed compared with typical expansion timelines in major North American sports. While exact internal deadlines were not disclosed, the report notes that the team’s ownership and basketball operations staff moved quickly through key steps: establishing the franchise identity, hiring a head coach, and beginning to assemble basketball and business infrastructure.

The Times identifies Joe Lacob, who also co-owns the NBA’s Golden State Warriors, as the driving force behind the Valkyries’ accelerated build-out. His group secured the franchise, committed to playing in the Bay Area, and pushed for a launch that would be visible to fans and sponsors in a matter of months rather than years. The article frames this pace as a central reason the team is being described as historic.

Head coach Natalie Nakase, a longtime assistant in the Warriors organization, was hired early in the process, according to the same report. That decision allowed basketball planning to begin alongside business and marketing work, rather than in a later phase. The Times describes Lacob and Nakase celebrating together as the organization hit early targets, underscoring how closely the basketball and business sides have been aligned during the launch.

## How the Valkyries made history

The New York Times report attributes the “history-making” label to several overlapping factors tied to speed and scale rather than a single record-book statistic. First, the Valkyries are one of the fastest-moving expansion efforts in recent WNBA history, the article indicates, with branding, leadership hires, and fan outreach all advancing in parallel.

Second, the franchise is backed by an NBA ownership group with an established record of transforming the Warriors into a high-revenue, high-visibility operation. The Times notes that this level of existing infrastructure—arena operations, marketing staff, and regional sponsorship relationships—allowed the Valkyries to plug into systems that most new teams must build from scratch.

Third, the report highlights how quickly the Valkyries have become visible within the Bay Area sports landscape. While specific attendance or sales figures were not detailed, the article points to early fan engagement and local attention as evidence that the team has moved from abstract concept to recognizable brand at a pace that stands out within the league.

The combination of these elements—compressed timelines, experienced ownership, and immediate market presence—is what the Times identifies as historically significant. The article does not claim a formal league record but presents the Valkyries’ launch as unprecedented in practical terms.

## The NBA connection and why it matters

The Valkyries’ close connection to the NBA’s Golden State Warriors is central to understanding how they moved so quickly, according to the New York Times. The Warriors’ ownership group, led by Lacob, already operates a major franchise in the same market, with established relationships across media, corporate sponsorship, and local government.

The report explains that this existing platform gave the Valkyries an unusual starting point. Instead of negotiating arena access, building a ticketing operation, and developing a brand from the ground up, the new team could draw on Warriors resources and expertise. That helped shorten the time between the WNBA’s expansion decision and a fully operational franchise.

From a competitive standpoint, the Times notes that this structure could influence how other potential WNBA expansion groups think about their own bids. While the article does not predict specific future moves, it suggests that the Valkyries’ rapid progress may serve as a reference point for how NBA-aligned ownership groups can enter or expand within women’s professional basketball.

## What is at stake for the league and fans

The New York Times report frames the Valkyries’ fast start as significant for three main groups: the WNBA, the Warriors’ organization, and fans in the Bay Area.

For the WNBA, the Valkyries represent a test of how quickly a new franchise can become competitive on and off the court when backed by an experienced NBA ownership group. The league has historically expanded cautiously, and the Times suggests that the Valkyries’ trajectory could influence how future expansion decisions are evaluated.

For the Warriors and Lacob, the article portrays the Valkyries as an extension of an existing sports business rather than a separate, experimental project. The speed of the launch is described as a deliberate choice to establish the team as a core part of the Bay Area’s professional sports lineup, not a secondary venture.

For fans, the report emphasizes access and visibility. By moving quickly on branding, coaching, and public-facing events, the Valkyries have given local supporters a tangible team to follow in a short period. The Times indicates that this immediacy—having a name, colors, leadership, and a clear connection to an existing NBA franchise—has helped generate early interest.

## Why this rapid launch matters now

The New York Times article presents the Golden State Valkyries as a case study in how a new professional team can leverage existing NBA infrastructure to make history through speed and scale. In one of the country’s most competitive sports markets, the franchise has moved from concept to reality fast enough to draw notice from league officials, rival teams, and fans.

While independent corroboration of every aspect of the team’s internal timelines is limited at this stage and will bear watching as more reporting emerges, the core facts reported by the Times are clear: the Valkyries have launched quickly, with visible backing from an established NBA ownership group, and are already being treated as a significant new presence in the Bay Area sports scene.

The next developments to watch, as implied by the Times coverage, will involve how this rapid start translates into on-court performance, sustained fan engagement, and potential ripple effects on how other NBA and WNBA stakeholders approach future expansion.
