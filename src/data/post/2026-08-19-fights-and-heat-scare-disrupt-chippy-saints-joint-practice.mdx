A joint practice involving the New Orleans Saints in Southern California was repeatedly halted after at least six on-field fights, including one that ended with wide receiver Ronnie Bell being carted off amid concerns over the intense heat, according to reporting from the Guardian. Defensive lineman Donovan Ezeiruaku was ejected from the session after throwing punches during one of the brawls.

The series of altercations turned what was meant to be a controlled training camp session into a stop‑start workout dominated by tempers and safety concerns, the Guardian reported.

## What Happened on the Field

The Guardian’s account describes a practice marked by escalating physical confrontations between players from the two teams. At least six separate fights broke out over the course of the session, repeatedly interrupting drills and forcing coaches and staff to step in.

During one of these skirmishes, New Orleans wide receiver Ronnie Bell became involved and later had to be driven from the field on a cart. The Guardian reported that Bell’s exit was linked to the effects of the Southern California heat rather than a specific contact injury from the fight itself.

In a separate incident, defensive lineman Donovan Ezeiruaku was ejected from practice after throwing punches during a brawl, according to the same report. Ejections in practice are typically handled by team staff rather than game officials, but the move underscored how far the session had drifted from routine training.

While joint practices are often intense, the volume of fights and the need to remove both an overheated player and a combatant who threw punches made this session stand out in the Guardian’s description.

## Heat Concerns After Bell Carted Off

The Guardian reported that Bell was “carted off the field because of the effects of Southern California heat” following his involvement in one of the fights. Being carted off is generally reserved for situations where a player cannot safely leave the field under his own power and allows medical staff to evaluate him away from the main practice area.

The report did not specify Bell’s exact symptoms, diagnosis, or how long he remained under observation. It also did not state whether he was later cleared to return to practice or play. Based on the available information, the key confirmed point is that the combination of high temperatures and the physical demands of a heated joint practice led to enough concern that staff decided to remove him by cart.

The Guardian’s account links Bell’s situation directly to the heat, rather than to a clearly documented injury from a hit or a fall during the fight. Without additional independent reports, further details about his condition, any treatment, or his expected timetable are not known.

## Ejection After Punches Thrown

The same Guardian report notes that Donovan Ezeiruaku was ejected from the practice after throwing punches during one of the brawls. In the context of a training camp session, an ejection typically means coaches or team officials instruct a player to leave the field and not participate in the remainder of the workout.

The report does not detail what triggered the confrontation involving Ezeiruaku, how long it lasted, or whether any other players were disciplined. It also does not describe any injuries resulting from the punches.

What is clear from the Guardian’s description is that the coaching staff or practice organizers considered the behavior serious enough to remove Ezeiruaku entirely, signaling an effort to regain control over a practice that had already featured multiple fights.

## Why the Practice Matters for the Saints

Joint practices are designed to give teams a chance to face unfamiliar opponents in a controlled setting, often seen by coaches as more useful than preseason games for evaluating players and systems. The Guardian’s reporting indicates that, for the Saints, this particular session was overshadowed by discipline and safety issues.

Multiple fights can disrupt planned drills, limit the number of useful snaps for coaches to evaluate, and increase the risk of avoidable injuries. The need to cart off Ronnie Bell due to heat effects adds another layer of concern, especially in a training environment where teams typically try to manage workloads and hydration to avoid heat‑related problems.

The Guardian report does not describe how coaches or team officials addressed the fights afterward, nor does it provide information on any formal disciplinary steps beyond Ezeiruaku’s ejection from the practice itself. It also does not detail any changes to subsequent practice plans.

## What to Watch Next

Based on the Guardian’s account, the key developments to monitor now relate to player health and team discipline:

- Whether the Saints or their opponents provide updates on Ronnie Bell’s condition and availability for future practices or games.
- Any public comments from coaches or team officials about the repeated fights and Ezeiruaku’s ejection.
- Signs in future practices or preseason action that the teams have adjusted how they run joint sessions in response to this incident.

With independent corroboration still limited to the Guardian’s event‑level reporting, additional coverage from other outlets may clarify the severity of Bell’s heat‑related issue, the internal response to the brawls, and any longer‑term implications for the Saints’ training camp.

For now, the confirmed picture is of a joint practice in Southern California where at least six fights, a heat‑related scare for Ronnie Bell, and the ejection of Donovan Ezeiruaku combined to overshadow the on‑field work coaches had planned.
