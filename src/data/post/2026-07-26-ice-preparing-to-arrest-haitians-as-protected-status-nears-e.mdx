U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is preparing to arrest Haitian migrants in Ohio and place them on rapid deportation flights to Haiti as their Temporary Protected Status (TPS) is set to expire, according to internal agency documents described by CBS News.

The plans, as reported by CBS News on May 10, outline targeted enforcement operations focused on Haitians whose TPS protections are ending, with the goal of quickly removing them to a country facing severe political and humanitarian turmoil.

## What the internal documents show

According to CBS News, internal ICE documents detail plans to locate and arrest Haitian nationals in Ohio who will lose TPS when the current designation expires. The records, as described by the outlet, indicate that those arrested would be placed on expedited deportation flights to Haiti.

CBS News reports that the documents describe a strategy of arresting migrants in the state and moving them swiftly through the removal process, rather than dispersing operations across multiple regions. The report does not specify the exact number of people ICE intends to target or the precise dates on which arrests or flights would occur.

The information about the planned operations comes from internal ICE materials and agency sources cited by CBS News. Independent corroboration of the specific operational details remains limited, and no separate public documentation has yet been made available to confirm the full scope of the plan.

## Temporary Protected Status for Haitians

Temporary Protected Status is a federal program that allows the U.S. government to grant temporary legal status and work authorization to foreign nationals from countries experiencing armed conflict, environmental disaster, or other extraordinary conditions. When TPS expires or is not renewed for a given country, beneficiaries can lose their protection from deportation.

In this case, CBS News reports that ICE planning is tied directly to the scheduled end of TPS protections for certain Haitians. As the current designation lapses, those who have not secured another form of legal status become subject to standard immigration enforcement, including arrest and removal.

The CBS News account links the timing of the planned Ohio operations to that TPS end date, describing the enforcement push as a response to the expiration of legal protections rather than a new, stand‑alone policy.

## Rapid removals to a crisis-stricken Haiti

The internal documents, as described by CBS News, call for placing arrested Haitians on removal flights to Haiti on an accelerated timeline. The outlet characterizes the country as “crisis‑stricken,” reflecting ongoing instability and humanitarian challenges there.

CBS News reports that the operational plan emphasizes speed, with a focus on moving people from arrest to deportation flights quickly. The report does not provide detailed information on how long individuals would be held in ICE custody before being placed on planes, nor does it describe any specific procedures for assessing individual circumstances prior to removal.

While the CBS News story highlights Haiti’s ongoing crisis as a central concern, it does not detail any changes to broader U.S. policy toward Haiti beyond the described enforcement actions linked to TPS expiration.

## Limited public detail and next steps

As of the CBS News report on May 10, ICE had not publicly released the internal documents described in the story, and the agency had not issued a detailed public announcement outlining the Ohio operations. The report relies on internal planning materials and agency sources to describe the anticipated arrests and flights.

CBS News notes that independent verification of the full plan remains limited, and the outlet’s account is currently the primary public description of the proposed enforcement actions. Key questions, including how many people may be affected, how long the operations may last, and whether similar plans exist in other states, are not fully addressed in the available reporting.

## Why this matters

If carried out as described, the ICE operations would mark a significant shift for Haitians in Ohio who are losing TPS protections, moving them from temporary legal status to potential rapid removal. The CBS News report indicates that federal immigration enforcement is preparing to act quickly as TPS expires, with direct consequences for those targeted for arrest and deportation.

Further developments to watch include any public confirmation or modification of the described plans by ICE or the Department of Homeland Security, additional reporting that corroborates or clarifies the scope of the operations, and any official decisions about TPS that could alter the timing or impact of enforcement actions.
