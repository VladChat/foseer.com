Mauricio Pochettino’s first competitive game as Manchester United head coach collapsed almost as soon as it began, as Liverpool capitalised on an early red card for defender Leny Yoro to hand their rivals a bruising defeat at Anfield.

Reporting from *the Guardian* describes an afternoon in which any sense of a new start for United was overwhelmed by the familiar sight of Liverpool dominating a badly outmatched visiting side. The match turned inside the opening 10 minutes, when Yoro was sent off, leaving Pochettino’s team to play nearly the entire game with 10 men.

## Anfield debut turns into early ordeal

Pochettino arrived at Old Trafford amid talk of “green shoots of recovery” for a club that has spent years trying to re-establish itself at the top of English football. Those hopes met a harsh reality on Merseyside.

According to *the Guardian’s* account of the game, Yoro’s dismissal came after only eight minutes. Reduced to 10 men away at Anfield, United quickly lost any chance of controlling the contest. Liverpool, already playing in front of a home crowd and facing a United side in transition, were handed a major advantage that shaped the rest of the match.

The report characterises the afternoon as “another abysmal Anfield day” for United, underlining how the venue has become a recurring site of heavy setbacks for the club. Pochettino’s debut, which might have been framed as a fresh chapter, instead became an exercise in damage limitation almost from the opening whistle.

## Liverpool seize the advantage

Once Yoro was sent off, Liverpool were able to dictate the tempo and territory. While the Guardian report focuses largely on the turning point of the red card and the broader pattern of United’s struggles at Anfield, it makes clear that Jürgen Klopp’s side — used here as shorthand for Liverpool’s established identity in recent seasons — took full advantage of their numerical superiority.

With United forced to reorganise defensively and sacrifice attacking ambition, Liverpool found space and time on the ball. The home side’s control of the game, as described in the coverage, reinforced a sense of continuity: Liverpool as a settled, confident team; United as a club still searching for stability even under a new manager.

## What the result means for Pochettino and United

The Guardian’s framing of the match highlights how quickly optimism around Pochettino’s appointment ran into trouble. The description that “green shoots of recovery have lasted longer in a blast-furnace than in the Mauricio Pochettino era at Manchester United” underlines the speed with which early hope gave way to familiar problems.

On a basic level, the result leaves United starting this phase of their season — and Pochettino’s tenure — on the back foot. A comprehensive defeat to Liverpool is damaging in sporting terms and symbolically significant for supporters who measure progress not just in points but in performances against major rivals.

The early red card complicates any simple reading of the game as a verdict on Pochettino’s methods. Playing almost the entire match with 10 men at Anfield is a severe test for any side. However, the Guardian report suggests that, even allowing for the dismissal, United’s display fed into an existing narrative of vulnerability in big away fixtures.

## Why this matters beyond one bad day

Although the available reporting focuses tightly on the match itself, the implications are clear enough for both clubs.

For Liverpool, the victory reinforces their position in direct rivalry with United and maintains Anfield’s status as an inhospitable ground for visiting teams, particularly those in flux. The Guardian’s emphasis on United’s recurring “abysmal” experiences there speaks to Liverpool’s continued ability to rise for these occasions.

For Manchester United, the game serves as an early stress test of Pochettino’s tenure. The new head coach must now respond to a high-profile setback that unfolded in front of a global audience and against the club’s most bitter domestic rival. While the red card provides context, the scale of Liverpool’s control after Yoro’s dismissal ensures the result will be seen as more than just bad luck.

Supporters and club decision‑makers will be watching to see how quickly United can stabilise performances after this defeat. The Guardian’s account of the match does not speculate on long‑term consequences, but by underlining how swiftly Pochettino’s debut turned into an ordeal, it captures the pressure that will accompany his next fixtures.

## What to watch next

Based on the Guardian’s reporting, the key questions now are how Pochettino adjusts his defensive setup after Yoro’s dismissal and whether United can show resilience in their upcoming games. For Liverpool, the task is to build on a statement performance at home and translate dominance in a marquee fixture into sustained consistency.

The match at Anfield will likely be remembered less for tactical nuance than for the moment, eight minutes in, when Yoro’s red card reshaped everything that followed. For Pochettino and Manchester United, it sets an early benchmark for adversity — and for how they respond under scrutiny in the weeks ahead.
