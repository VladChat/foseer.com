London’s Metropolitan Police seized an estimated $10 million worth of high-end supercars over a single weekend, including a rare Ferrari Monza SP2 that can sell for as much as $5 million at auction, according to reporting by CBS News. The operation marks one of the largest short-term impounds of ultra-luxury vehicles in the city, based on the values cited in that coverage.

While the CBS News report does not list every make and model, it attributes the overall value and the presence of the Monza SP2 to information from London police, underscoring the scale and unusual nature of the weekend sweep.

## What police say happened

According to CBS News’ account of the incident, London’s Metropolitan Police carried out enforcement activity over a single weekend that resulted in the impounding of multiple supercars whose combined value was estimated at about $10 million.

Among the vehicles seized was a Ferrari Monza SP2, a limited-production, open-top sports car. CBS News notes that this model has fetched up to around $5 million at auction, meaning a single car accounted for roughly half the total value of the weekend’s seizures.

CBS News reports that the vehicles were taken into police custody in London, though the article does not specify exact streets or districts. The outlet attributes the valuation and the identification of the Ferrari model to police information and auction records, but it does not publish a full official list of all impounded cars.

The CBS News report does not detail the precise legal grounds for each individual seizure. In the United Kingdom, vehicles can be impounded for reasons that include driving without insurance, suspected links to criminal activity, or violations of traffic or registration rules. However, the article does not attribute any one of these specific reasons to the cars seized in this operation, and it does not describe any related criminal charges.

## The Ferrari Monza SP2 and its significance

CBS News highlights the Ferrari Monza SP2 as the standout vehicle among those impounded. The Monza SP2 is described in the report as a highly exclusive model that has sold for as much as $5 million at auction.

That figure, cited by CBS News, is based on auction results rather than a police valuation, and it helps explain why the weekend’s total estimated value reached $10 million despite the limited number of cars involved. Even without a complete inventory of the seized vehicles, the presence of a single car with a multimillion-dollar auction track record sets this operation apart from typical traffic or insurance-related impounds.

The CBS report does not identify the owner of the Monza SP2, nor does it specify the country of registration or any corporate or individual entity linked to the vehicle.
