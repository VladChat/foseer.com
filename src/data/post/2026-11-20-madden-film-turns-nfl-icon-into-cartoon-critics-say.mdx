Nicolas Cage’s latest role as legendary NFL coach and broadcaster John Madden is drawing sharp criticism, with one early review calling the new film a “thoroughly unserious” biopic that reduces a complex sports figure to a cartoon.

According to a review published by the Guardian, the film — directed by Oscar-nominated filmmaker David O Russell — squanders a heavyweight cast, including Cage, Christian Bale and Sienna Miller, on what the critic describes as a misjudged, tonally erratic portrait of one of American football’s most recognisable figures.

## A larger-than-life coach played as pure caricature

John Madden, who died in 2021, was known in the United States as a Super Bowl–winning coach, a wildly popular television analyst and the namesake of the long-running Madden NFL video game franchise. The Guardian review notes that the film leans heavily into his outsized public persona but, in doing so, flattens him into a one-note figure.

The critic characterises Cage’s performance as “cartoonish,” arguing that the actor, under Russell’s direction, plays Madden almost entirely for broad laughs. Instead of exploring the pressures of coaching at the highest level or the transition to broadcasting, the film reportedly opts for exaggerated mannerisms and comic set-pieces that treat Madden’s quirks as punchlines rather than windows into his character.

This approach, the review suggests, leaves little room for nuance. Madden’s well-documented fear of flying, his intense work ethic and his role in helping popularise the NFL for television audiences are touched on, but largely as background gags or shorthand, rather than as threads in a deeper narrative.

## Star power, thin material

The Guardian describes the film as a “stinker” in spite of its cast, arguing that Russell gives his actors little to work with beyond surface-level traits. Christian Bale and Sienna Miller, both praised in past collaborations with the director, are said to be underused here.

Bale, cast in a key supporting role, is portrayed in the review as trapped in a script that offers him few dimensions beyond reacting to Madden’s antics. Miller, playing one of the central figures in Madden’s personal life, is described as similarly constrained by writing that sketches relationships in shorthand rather than building them over time.

The review positions this as a broader problem with the film’s structure. Rather than tracing a clear emotional or professional arc, the story jumps between episodes in Madden’s life — from the sidelines to the broadcast booth and beyond — without, in the critic’s view, establishing what is at stake for him in each phase. The result, the Guardian argues, is a biopic that feels busy but emotionally weightless.

## A director with pedigree, a film with little discipline

David O Russell has previously received multiple Academy Award nominations and is known for character-driven films that mix comedy and drama. The Guardian’s assessment of *Madden* suggests that this project lacks the discipline and balance that defined his strongest work.

According to the review, Russell leans into slapstick and heightened dialogue at the expense of coherence. Scenes that might have explored the changing business of the NFL, the demands of coaching, or the pressures of sudden celebrity instead become showcases for eccentric behavior and rapid-fire banter.

The critic argues that this tonal choice undercuts the film’s potential to say anything meaningful about Madden’s influence on football culture or sports broadcasting. Instead of using humor to illuminate character, the film is described as using character as a vehicle for jokes, leaving viewers with a series of loud moments rather than a sustained portrait.

## Why this portrayal matters

While the Guardian review focuses primarily on artistic choices, it also hints at why the film’s approach matters beyond the screen. Madden’s name is deeply embedded in contemporary football culture, from the long-running EA Sports video game series to decades of NFL broadcasts.

By presenting Madden as a broadly comic figure, the film risks, in the critic’s view, reducing a pivotal sports personality to a caricature for audiences who may know him only from the game cover or from clips circulating on platforms like TikTok. The review suggests that this could shape how younger fans, who did not see Madden coach or call games live, understand his legacy.

At the same time, the Guardian notes that the film’s failure to grapple with Madden’s role in the NFL’s rise — including how television, marketing and personality-driven coverage helped turn the league into a dominant entertainment force — represents a missed opportunity. Those elements are present in the background, the critic writes, but rarely examined with any depth.

## A divisive entry in the sports-biopic genre

The Guardian’s early verdict situates *Madden* as a misfire in a genre that has recently produced both crowd-pleasing hits and more probing character studies. Where some sports biopics use the field or court as a stage for broader questions about identity, ambition or power, this film is described as content to skim the surface of a well-known brand.

The review does not claim to speak for all critics or audiences, and independent corroboration of its assessment is limited so far. However, its detailed account of the film’s tone, structure and performances offers an early indication that *Madden* may divide viewers who come expecting either a serious character study or a balanced blend of drama and comedy.

For now, the Guardian’s appraisal frames the film as a high-profile but shallow take on a figure who helped define modern American football. How other critics and fans respond — and whether they see Cage’s performance as affectionate exaggeration or empty caricature — will shape the conversation as the film reaches a wider audience.
