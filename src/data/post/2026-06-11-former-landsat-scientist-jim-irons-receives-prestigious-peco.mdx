NASA has announced that Jim Irons, former project scientist for the Landsat program, has been selected to receive the William T. Pecora Award, a major honor in the field of Earth observation.

The agency described the award as recognition of Irons’ long-running contributions to satellite-based monitoring of the planet, particularly through his leadership role on Landsat, according to a NASA science update published on its website.

## What NASA Announced

In its report, NASA identified Jim Irons as a former Landsat project scientist and stated that he is this year’s recipient of the William T. Pecora Award. The announcement positions the award as a significant professional recognition within the remote sensing community, which focuses on gathering information about Earth from satellites and aircraft.

The NASA account links Irons’ recognition directly to his work on Landsat, the long‑running series of Earth‑observing satellites managed by NASA and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). While the agency’s update centers on the award itself, it also underscores that Landsat data have become a core tool for monitoring changes on Earth’s surface over time.

## Who Jim Irons Is and His Role in Landsat

NASA’s description of Jim Irons emphasizes his role as a former Landsat project scientist. In NASA’s program structure, a project scientist typically serves as the primary scientific lead for a mission, helping define what measurements the satellite will make, how instruments are used, and how scientific priorities are balanced with engineering and operational constraints.

By highlighting Irons’ former title, NASA is tying the Pecora Award directly to his leadership within the Landsat program. The agency’s account credits his work with helping guide the mission’s scientific direction, which in turn shapes how Landsat observations are used by researchers, government agencies, and other users.

## What the William T. Pecora Award Represents

NASA’s announcement identifies the honor given to Irons as the William T. Pecora Award. The award is widely regarded in the Earth observation community as a high‑level recognition for contributions to satellite‑based monitoring of the planet. It is named for William T. Pecora, a key early advocate for using space‑based imaging to study Earth.

While NASA’s update focuses on Irons as the recipient, the agency’s framing indicates that the award is meant to highlight individuals whose work has advanced the use of remote sensing in understanding Earth’s land surface. By selecting a former Landsat project scientist, the award committee is drawing attention to the importance of long‑term, consistent satellite records.

## Why the Award Matters for Earth Observation

In its write‑up, NASA links Irons’ Pecora Award to the broader impact of Landsat. The agency notes that Landsat satellites provide sustained, calibrated images of Earth’s land areas. These observations are used in many areas of Earth science and resource management, including tracking land‑use change, observing forests and agriculture, and monitoring water bodies.

By recognizing Irons, NASA is effectively underscoring the value of scientific leadership in maintaining and improving this long‑term record. The agency’s account suggests that the Pecora Award, in this case, is not only about an individual career but also about the continued importance of systematic, satellite‑based observation of Earth’s surface.
