The Premier League’s opening weekend delivered a familiar rush of noise, scrutiny and early verdicts on clubs that have spent the summer rebuilding. Across the first round of fixtures, attention settled on Manchester United’s lack of cutting edge, a standout value signing at Brentford, and a decisive display from Kieran Dewsbury-Hall for Everton, according to analysis from the Guardian’s match reports and weekend roundup.

While one round of games rarely defines a season, the performances and selection calls offered early evidence of where squads look prepared – and where problems may linger.

## Manchester United’s attack under the microscope

Manchester United emerged from their first match with renewed questions about their attacking options. Reporting from the Guardian’s opening-weekend coverage noted that United’s forward line looked under-powered, with long spells of possession failing to translate into clear chances.

The concern is not new. Last season, United’s goal return lagged behind the Premier League’s leading sides, and the club’s summer business has so far not fully addressed the need for a consistent goal threat. The opening fixture reinforced that gap: United’s build-up play was often neat, but their final ball and movement in the box lacked the sharpness on display at rivals who have invested heavily in attacking depth.

Coaching staff have publicly stressed patience with younger forwards and recent signings, but the early evidence, as described in the Guardian’s analysis, points to a squad still short of proven end-product in the final third. With the transfer window open, the performance is likely to intensify external pressure on recruitment decisions.

## Brentford’s £39m signing looks an early bargain

At Brentford, a new signing reported at around £39m drew attention for the right reasons. The Guardian’s weekend talking points highlighted the player as a potential bargain at that fee, pointing to an immediate impact in open play and a quick adaptation to Thomas Frank’s system.

Brentford have built a reputation for data-led recruitment and for extracting value from players outside the traditional elite markets. Context from Front Office Sports and CNBC on the Premier League’s global commercial strength underscores how clubs like Brentford, while operating on smaller budgets than the league’s giants, can still deploy significant fees when they believe the underlying numbers support it.

On the opening weekend, that calculation appeared justified. The new arrival’s movement and pressing aligned with Brentford’s high-energy approach, and early combinations with existing attackers suggested the signing could reshape the team’s attacking balance. It is too early to label any transfer a definitive success, but the first impression matched the club’s reputation for shrewd business.

## Dewsbury-Hall delivers for Everton

Everton’s start to the season carried particular weight after recent relegation battles and financial constraints. In that context, Kieran Dewsbury-Hall’s performance stood out. The Guardian’s summary of the weekend listed him among the most influential players of the first round of fixtures, highlighting his contribution as a key factor in Everton’s display.

Dewsbury-Hall, who arrived this summer after previously establishing himself as a reliable midfielder elsewhere in English football, offered both energy and composure. Match reporting noted his ability to link play between defence and attack, as well as his willingness to take responsibility in possession.

For Everton, whose recent seasons have been marked by inconsistency and a lack of creativity in midfield, his early impact is significant. While one game cannot resolve structural issues at the club, Dewsbury-Hall’s showing gave supporters and coaching staff a concrete example of how targeted recruitment might stabilise performances.

## Arsenal selection raises questions over Martinelli

One of the weekend’s most debated selection calls came at Arsenal, where Gabriel Martinelli was left out of the squad for Friday’s opening match. The Guardian’s coverage reported that the Brazil forward had turned down a move to Turkish side Galatasaray after Arsenal had agreed a fee with the Istanbul club.

Former Arsenal striker Ian Wright publicly questioned the decision to omit Martinelli, according to the Guardian’s account of post-match reaction. Wright’s comments reflected wider uncertainty among supporters about how the club plans to use a player who has previously been central to Mikel Arteta’s attacking plans.

The situation, as described in the reporting, involves several moving parts: Arsenal’s willingness to accept a bid, Martinelli’s choice to reject the transfer, and Arteta’s subsequent decision not to include him in the matchday squad. None of the parties has yet set out a detailed public explanation of the long-term plan, leaving room for interpretation but few confirmed facts beyond the selection itself and the rejected move.

From a squad-management perspective, the decision matters because it touches on depth in wide attacking positions and on the balance between established starters and emerging options. With Arsenal again expected to compete at the top end of the table, the handling of a high-profile forward so early in the season is likely to remain a talking point until his role is clarified by future team sheets and any further transfer developments.

## Early patterns and what they can – and cannot – tell us

Across the opening weekend, the Guardian’s 10-point roundup stressed that early conclusions must be tempered by the small sample size. Fitness levels vary, new signings are still settling, and some clubs are managing injuries or late-arriving internationals.

Contextual reporting from Front Office Sports and CNBC has highlighted how the Premier League’s global profile and financial scale intensify scrutiny on even the first round of matches. Clubs operate in an environment where every performance feeds into narratives about spending, coaching decisions and long-term strategy, even when the underlying evidence is still thin.

Still, certain themes from the first set of fixtures are clear enough to note. Teams that maintained continuity over the summer generally looked more cohesive. Sides with major rebuilds in key areas, such as attack, showed more inconsistency. Individual decisions – like Arsenal’s handling of Martinelli – acquired outsized attention because they signal possible shifts in internal hierarchies.

Analysts quoted in the Guardian’s coverage consistently cautioned against overreacting. Yet they also pointed out that early points can shape the trajectory of a season, especially for clubs under pressure to qualify for European competitions or avoid another relegation fight.

## What to watch over the next few days

In the next 24 to 72 hours, attention is likely to focus on how managers respond publicly to the questions raised by the opening weekend. Press conferences ahead of the second round of fixtures are expected to bring further comments on selection issues, including Arsenal’s stance on Martinelli and Manchester United’s assessment of their attacking options.

Transfer activity could also sharpen some of these talking points. With the window still open, clubs that looked short in key areas may move to accelerate deals, and any confirmed bids or signings would quickly alter the context of early performances highlighted in the Guardian’s reporting.

Supporters and analysts will be watching team news for the next set of matches to see whether opening-weekend patterns hold. If managers double down on their initial selections, it may signal confidence in existing plans; significant changes could indicate that some early concerns, such as United’s lack of cutting edge or Arsenal’s wide options, have been taken on board.
