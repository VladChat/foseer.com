Kazuo Ishiguro’s next novel, a 1930s-set spy caper titled *Miss Lambert Steps Aboard Danger*, is scheduled for publication in March next year, according to an announcement from his UK publisher Faber reported by the Guardian.

The book will be Ishiguro’s first novel since *Klara and the Sun* was released in 2021. Faber’s announcement, as described in the Guardian’s coverage, positions the work as a departure in tone, drawing on the author’s enthusiasm for music, visual art and Golden Age cinema.

## A new Ishiguro novel set in 1938

Faber has described *Miss Lambert Steps Aboard Danger* as a spy caper set in 1938, the Guardian reports. The story follows a character named Richard Hadley, although detailed plot information has not yet been fully released.

The publisher’s description, as relayed in the Guardian article, highlights the novel’s period setting on the eve of the Second World War and its engagement with the style and atmosphere of 1930s popular entertainment. The reference to Golden Age cinema points to the classic Hollywood era, typically understood to span the late 1920s through the 1940s, known for tightly structured genre films, including thrillers and spy stories.

According to the Guardian’s account of the Faber announcement, Ishiguro’s new book is framed as a “spy caper,” a term generally used in publishing and film to signal a plot-driven story involving espionage, risk and often a lighter or adventurous tone. Beyond the 1938 setting and the focus on Richard Hadley, no further confirmed storyline details were provided in the coverage.

## First novel since *Klara and the Sun*

The Guardian notes that *Miss Lambert Steps Aboard Danger* will be Ishiguro’s first novel since *Klara and the Sun*, which came out in 2021. That earlier book, also published by Faber in the UK, followed an artificial friend designed to be a child’s companion and examined questions of consciousness and care.

The three-year gap between novels is in line with Ishiguro’s established pattern of taking several years between major works. The Guardian article situates the new title as a notable literary event, given Ishiguro’s status as a Nobel laureate and the attention his recent fiction has received from critics and prize juries.

## Drawing on music, art and Golden Age cinema

Faber’s announcement, as reported by the Guardian, says the novel draws on Ishiguro’s love of music, art and Golden Age cinema. The publisher’s emphasis suggests that these influences may shape the book’s style, structure or atmosphere, though the specific ways they appear in the narrative were not detailed in the coverage.

Music and visual art have figured in Ishiguro’s work before, including novels that feature musicians or artists as central characters. The Guardian’s description of the publisher’s statement indicates that this new book will again foreground those interests, this time within the framework of a 1930s spy story.

The mention of Golden Age cinema in the announcement, as relayed by the Guardian, signals that the novel may echo the pacing, visual sensibility or genre conventions of films from that period. However, without further material from the publisher or the author, the exact nature of that influence remains unspecified.

## Why the announcement matters

The Guardian’s report on Faber’s announcement underscores the significance of a new novel from Ishiguro for readers, booksellers and the broader cultural conversation around contemporary fiction. As a Nobel laureate whose work is widely translated and adapted, any new title from Ishiguro is likely to attract international attention and substantial pre-publication interest.

The 1938 spy-caper premise marks a contrast with the near-future and alternative-historical settings of some of his recent novels, a shift the Guardian coverage highlights by noting the period backdrop and genre framing described by Faber. For readers and critics, this sets expectations of a work that combines Ishiguro’s established themes with a more overtly suspenseful or playful narrative mode.

## What is known and what is still to come

At this stage, the key confirmed details, as reported by the Guardian from Faber’s announcement, are the title, *Miss Lambert Steps Aboard Danger*; the 1938 setting; the description of the book as a spy caper; the central character, Richard Hadley; the influence of music, art and Golden Age cinema; and the planned publication in March next year.

Independent corroboration beyond the Guardian’s reporting on Faber’s announcement is currently limited and will be important to monitor as additional information is released by the publisher, the author or other outlets. Further details about the plot, international publication dates and promotional plans are likely to emerge closer to the release.

For now, the announcement signals that readers can expect a new, period-set espionage story from Kazuo Ishiguro in early 2025, with Faber presenting it as a significant addition to his body of work and a notable event on next year’s literary calendar.
