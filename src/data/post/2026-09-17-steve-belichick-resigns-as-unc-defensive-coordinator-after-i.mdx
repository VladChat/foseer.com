North Carolina defensive coordinator Steve Belichick has resigned from his role after the university concluded an internal investigation into turmoil surrounding the football program, according to reporting from the Guardian. The inquiry found that “certain actions” associated with the program ran counter to the school’s stated values, the outlet reported.

Belichick, the son of head coach Bill Belichick, becomes the second senior football figure to depart in a matter of weeks, following the earlier resignation of general manager Michael Lombardi.

## What the investigation found

According to the Guardian’s account of the university’s findings, the internal investigation determined that unnamed “certain actions” linked to the football program did not align with North Carolina’s principles. The report did not specify which individuals were responsible for those actions or describe them in detail.

The Guardian’s reporting indicates that the university framed the issue in terms of values and conduct rather than on-field performance. The language suggests a focus on behavior or decision-making within the program, but with only one outlet providing details so far, the exact nature of the conduct remains unclear.

Because independent corroboration is limited at this stage, the public picture of the investigation’s scope and findings is based largely on how the Guardian describes the school’s conclusions. No further documentation or statements were cited in that coverage.

## Steve Belichick’s role and resignation

Steve Belichick had been serving as North Carolina’s defensive coordinator under his father, Bill Belichick, who is head coach of the team. The Guardian reports that Steve Belichick stepped down as the school wrapped up its review of the football program.

The timing, as described in that reporting, links his resignation directly to the conclusion of the inquiry. However, the Guardian coverage does not quote Steve Belichick or university officials explaining whether he resigned voluntarily, was asked to step aside, or reached a mutual agreement with the school.

The article also does not detail any disciplinary measures beyond his departure, nor does it state that he was personally found to have violated policies. Instead, it connects his exit to the broader finding that some actions around the program were inconsistent with institutional values.

## Earlier resignation of GM Michael Lombardi

The Guardian further reports that Michael Lombardi, the program’s general manager, resigned earlier in the month, before Steve Belichick’s departure. His exit is presented as part of the same period of upheaval around North Carolina football, though the article does not specify whether Lombardi’s resignation was directly tied to the investigation’s conclusions.

The sequence, as laid out by the Guardian, is that Lombardi left first, then the school finalized its inquiry, and Steve Belichick resigned as defensive coordinator as that process ended. No additional details are provided about Lombardi’s role in the issues identified by the school, or whether his departure was characterized internally as disciplinary, strategic, or personal.

## Why this matters for UNC football

Based on the Guardian’s reporting, North Carolina’s football program has now lost both its defensive coordinator and its general manager within a short span, against the backdrop of an internal investigation into conduct and values. For any college program, turnover in these positions can affect recruiting, game planning, and day-to-day operations.

The Guardian notes that Steve Belichick is the son of head coach Bill Belichick, a connection that has drawn added attention to the situation. Changes involving close staff relationships at the top of a program can raise questions about how responsibilities will be reassigned and how the staff dynamic will shift.

At this point, the Guardian’s account does not indicate how North Carolina plans to fill the defensive coordinator or general manager roles, or whether the school will release more detail about the investigation’s findings.

## What to watch next

With only one primary outlet, the Guardian, providing detailed reporting so far, the available information about the investigation and its consequences remains limited. Key open questions include:

- Whether North Carolina will publish a fuller account of the inquiry’s findings
- How the school will restructure its football staff after the departures of Steve Belichick and Michael Lombardi
- Whether additional reporting from other organizations will confirm, expand, or clarify the Guardian’s description of the turmoil

For now, the confirmed developments are that North Carolina’s internal review concluded that some actions around the football program conflicted with school values, and that both the defensive coordinator, Steve Belichick, and general manager, Michael Lombardi, have resigned in close succession. How the program responds to that disruption will shape its next steps on and off the field.
