Hurricane Polo has intensified into a powerful Category 4 storm off Mexico’s southwestern coast, according to reporting from CBS News based on official storm monitoring data. The hurricane is churning over the eastern Pacific Ocean, away from land for now, but its strength and proximity have put coastal regions on alert as forecasters track its path.

CBS News reports that Polo’s intensity has fluctuated between Category 4 and the threshold of Category 5 — the highest rating on the Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale, which classifies storms based on sustained wind speeds. That scale runs from Category 1 (least intense) to Category 5 (most intense), and Category 4 storms are capable of causing extreme wind damage if they make landfall.

## What we know about Hurricane Polo right now

CBS News describes Hurricane Polo as a “powerful Category 4 storm” located off Mexico’s southwestern coastline in the Pacific. While the exact coordinates, wind speeds, and central pressure figures are not detailed in the available reporting, the Category 4 designation indicates sustained winds of at least 130 miles per hour (209 kilometers per hour).

The storm’s position “off Mexico” places it over open water rather than directly over land, but its strength means that even a shift in track closer to the coast could significantly change the risk profile for communities along the shoreline. CBS News notes that Polo’s intensity has at times pushed toward Category 5, underscoring how volatile major hurricanes can be as they move over warm ocean waters.

At this stage, the primary focus of meteorologists, as relayed in the CBS News coverage, is on monitoring Polo’s path and intensity changes. The storm’s classification and location are confirmed; more detailed projections about land impacts, rainfall totals, or storm surge risks are not fully laid out in the current reporting.

## How the hurricane scale explains Polo’s strength

CBS News frames Hurricane Polo’s strength using the Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale, a widely used system in the Americas for categorizing tropical cyclones in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific.

On this scale:

- **Category 1–2** storms are considered hurricanes but generally cause less severe structural damage.
- **Category 3–5** storms are classified as *major hurricanes* because of their potential for extensive destruction.

A **Category 4** hurricane like Polo is near the top of that range. While CBS News does not list specific wind speeds for Polo, the Category 4 label itself is based on measured or estimated sustained winds of 130–156 mph (209–251 km/h). The report’s note that Polo has fluctuated near Category 5 suggests that, at times, its winds may have approached or briefly exceeded the 157 mph (252 km/h) threshold that defines the most intense storms.

In practice, a hurricane’s category is a shorthand for wind danger. It does not directly measure rainfall or storm surge, but stronger storms often bring heavier rain and higher coastal flooding. The CBS News reporting centers on Polo’s category and location, which are the clearest confirmed indicators of its current threat level.

According to CBS News, Hurricane Polo is currently offshore in the eastern Pacific, to the southwest of mainland Mexico. That offshore position means the storm is not making landfall at the moment, but it also means it is drawing energy from warm ocean waters that can help sustain or increase its intensity.
