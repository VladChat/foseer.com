Andrew Garfield’s latest historical drama, *The Uprising*, arrives with the weight of a star-led period epic but has been greeted as a disappointingly generic film. In a review published by the Guardian, the film is described as a “muddled historical epic” in which “the peasants are revolting,” and Garfield’s performance is judged unable to rescue the material from its grim tone and familiar storytelling.

The Guardian’s review frames *The Uprising* as another attempt by a major Hollywood-backed production to build a prestige vehicle around a young lead actor, a pattern the critic notes has not always translated into audience enthusiasm. The film is directed by Paul Greengrass, known for kinetic, politically tinged dramas, but the review argues that his approach here results in a bleak and conventional period piece rather than a distinctive historical portrait.

## A historical revolt with unfocused storytelling

According to the Guardian’s account, *The Uprising* is built around a peasant rebellion, using the structure of a large-scale historical epic to follow ordinary people pushed toward revolt. The review’s headline, calling the film “muddled,” signals that the critic found the storytelling diffuse and the themes underdeveloped.

The critic reports that the narrative attempts to balance personal drama with sweeping historical events but concludes that the film never fully commits to either. As described in the review, the plot moves through key beats of oppression, mobilization and confrontation, yet does so in a way that feels generic rather than rooted in specific historical or cultural detail.

This lack of focus, the Guardian suggests, leaves the uprising itself feeling more like a backdrop than a sharply drawn conflict. The review notes that the peasants’ grievances and motivations are sketched in broad strokes, which weakens the impact of the eventual clashes and dilutes the sense of stakes.

## Andrew Garfield’s performance and star-driven expectations

The Guardian review emphasizes Andrew Garfield’s central role and charisma, describing him as a clear focal point for the film’s marketing and dramatic ambitions. However, the critic argues that even his committed performance cannot overcome what is characterized as a thinly developed script and a heavy, joyless tone.

The piece links *The Uprising* to a broader Hollywood pattern: giving rising or established young actors large-scale historical vehicles that are meant to showcase their range. The review notes that while studios continue to pursue this strategy, audiences have not consistently embraced such projects, especially when the films rely on familiar narrative formulas.

In this case, the Guardian suggests that Garfield’s presence raises expectations the film does not meet. The review portrays him as doing solid work within the role but constrained by dialogue and character development that do not allow for a fully compelling arc. As a result, his star power is seen as highlighting the film’s shortcomings rather than masking them.

## Paul Greengrass’s direction under scrutiny

Director Paul Greengrass has a reputation for urgent, handheld camerawork and politically charged stories. The Guardian’s review indicates that some of his stylistic trademarks appear in *The Uprising*, but argues that they do not translate into a coherent or emotionally engaging epic.

The critic contends that Greengrass’s approach here leans heavily on grimness and intensity without offering enough narrative clarity or character depth. Battle scenes and crowd sequences are described as technically competent yet lacking the sense of immediacy and purpose that marked his earlier work.

According to the review, this mismatch between style and substance contributes to the film’s “depressingly generic” feel. The uprising is presented with seriousness and scale, but the Guardian finds that the direction does not carve out a distinctive point of view on the events or the people involved.

## A generic epic in a crowded field

The Guardian situates *The Uprising* within a crowded field of historical dramas and period epics, many of which compete for attention on major streaming platforms and in cinemas. In that context, the review argues, the film struggles to justify its length and scope.

The critic notes that the production values and cast suggest a prestige project, yet the film’s execution is said to rely on familiar tropes of rebellion stories: downtrodden villagers, brutal authority figures and inevitable confrontation. Without sharper characterization or a more original framing of the revolt, the Guardian concludes that *The Uprising* blends into a long line of similar titles rather than standing out.

The review also underlines a gap between ambition and impact. While the film appears to aim for a sweeping, emotionally resonant account of ordinary people pushed to extremes, the critic finds that the result is more schematic than stirring.

## Why this reception matters

The Guardian’s negative assessment of *The Uprising* matters for several reasons. First, it shapes early critical perception of a high-profile collaboration between Andrew Garfield and Paul Greengrass, two figures whose names often draw attention from audiences and platforms.

Second, the review highlights ongoing challenges for historical epics in the current entertainment landscape. When a film with recognizable talent and significant resources is still judged “muddled” and “generic,” it raises questions about how such projects are developed and positioned, and whether star casting alone is enough to attract and satisfy viewers.

Independent corroboration of the Guardian’s specific criticisms is limited so far, and broader critical consensus has not yet been firmly established. As more reviews and audience responses appear, they will clarify whether *The Uprising* is widely seen as a misfire or whether other viewers find more to value in its depiction of revolt and resistance.

For now, the Guardian’s review stands as an early, influential signal that this latest historical epic, despite its cast and ambitions, may struggle to make a lasting mark.
