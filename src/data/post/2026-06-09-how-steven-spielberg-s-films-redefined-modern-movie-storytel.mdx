Steven Spielberg’s films have helped define what many viewers think of as a modern Hollywood movie, from large-scale adventure to intimate historical drama. A survey of his work published by CBS News on May 10 highlights how his films, released over more than five decades, have influenced popular culture and commercial filmmaking.

CBS News notes that Spielberg’s career spans early thrillers such as *Jaws* to later historical dramas including *Schindler’s List*, with his films often setting box-office benchmarks and shaping audience expectations for mainstream cinema.

## Early Breakthroughs and the Birth of the Modern Blockbuster

According to CBS News, Spielberg’s major breakthrough came with *Jaws* (1975), a thriller about a great white shark terrorizing a beach town. The film’s wide release strategy and strong ticket sales are widely credited by film historians with helping to establish the summer “blockbuster” model, in which studios concentrate marketing and distribution around a few large-scale releases.

The CBS News overview places *Jaws* alongside *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* (1977) as early examples of Spielberg’s ability to combine genre storytelling with a focus on ordinary people confronting extraordinary events. In *Close Encounters*, a science-fiction story about contact with extraterrestrial life, Spielberg centered the narrative on a working-class family, a choice that CBS News notes became a hallmark of his approach in later films.

## Defining 1980s Adventure and Family Films

CBS News identifies the 1980s as a period when Spielberg’s films helped set the tone for adventure and family-oriented cinema. *Raiders of the Lost Ark* (1981), created with producer George Lucas, introduced archaeologist Indiana Jones and revived the style of early 20th-century adventure serials with updated visual effects and pacing.

The CBS News piece highlights *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial* (1982) as another key work from this era. The film, about a young boy who befriends an alien stranded on Earth, combined science fiction with a focus on childhood and family relationships. CBS News credits *E.T.* with reinforcing Spielberg’s reputation for blending spectacle with emotional storytelling, a pattern that would continue in later projects.

## Shifting to Historical Drama and War Stories

As summarized by CBS News, Spielberg’s career broadened in the 1990s to include more explicit historical and war-themed films. *Schindler’s List* (1993), a black-and-white drama about German industrialist Oskar Schindler’s efforts to save Jews during the Holocaust, marked a significant shift in tone from many of his earlier commercial hits.

CBS News notes that *Schindler’s List* is frequently cited by critics and historians as one of Spielberg’s most important works, both for its subject matter and for its restrained visual style compared with his more effects-driven films. The same year, he released *Jurassic Park*, a science-fiction adventure about cloned dinosaurs, underscoring the range of genres he worked in during the same period.

Later, *Saving Private Ryan* (1998) continued this historical focus with a depiction of World War II combat. CBS News points to the film’s opening D-Day sequence as especially influential, with its handheld camerawork and sound design often referenced by filmmakers and critics when discussing modern depictions of battle.

## Continued Experiments Across Genres

The CBS News overview emphasizes that Spielberg has repeatedly shifted genres rather than staying with a single style. In the 2000s and beyond, his films have included science fiction, crime stories, and further historical dramas.

CBS News cites *Minority Report* (2002), a science-fiction thriller about predictive policing, and *Catch Me If You Can* (2002), based on the life of con artist Frank Abagnale Jr., as examples of this range. Both films, released the same year, show Spielberg moving between futuristic settings and period crime drama while maintaining a focus on character-driven narratives.

The CBS News survey also mentions later historical films such as *Munich* (2005), about the aftermath of the 1972 Munich Olympics attack, and *Lincoln* (2012), which focuses on U.S. President Abraham Lincoln’s efforts to pass the 13th Amendment. These projects, according to CBS News, demonstrate Spielberg’s ongoing interest in political and moral questions framed through specific historical events.

## Influence on Audiences and the Film Industry

While CBS News concentrates on the films themselves, the piece also notes that Spielberg’s work has had a lasting impact on how studios think about large-scale releases and franchise-building. Films like *Jaws*, *E.T.*, *Jurassic Park*, and the *Indiana Jones* series are repeatedly cited in industry discussions about merchandising, sequels, and theme park attractions.

CBS News points out that Spielberg’s films often balance commercial appeal with distinctive personal themes, including family dynamics, moral responsibility, and the tension between wonder and fear. This combination, the outlet notes, has made his work a frequent subject of study in film schools and critical writing.

## Why His Filmography Still Matters

The CBS News review of Spielberg’s films underscores that his body of work continues to shape both audience expectations and studio strategies. Many of his titles remain in regular circulation through streaming platforms, television broadcasts, and theatrical re-releases, keeping them visible to new viewers.

Because the CBS News account is focused on his released films and their reception, it does not make detailed claims about future projects or broader political implications. It does, however, present a consistent picture of a filmmaker whose work has influenced how large-scale movies are produced, marketed, and discussed.

For readers tracking how contemporary cinema has evolved, the films outlined in the CBS News survey—from *Jaws* and *E.T.* to *Schindler’s List*, *Saving Private Ryan*, and beyond—offer a concrete map of how one director’s choices helped define what mainstream filmmaking looks and feels like today.
