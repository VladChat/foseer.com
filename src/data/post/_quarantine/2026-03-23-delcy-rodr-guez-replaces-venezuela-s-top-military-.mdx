Delcy Rodríguez overhauls Venezuela’s top military command as armed forces pledge loyalty.

Delcy Rodríguez, Venezuela’s interim president, has removed the country’s top military commanders and installed new leadership at the head of the armed forces, a rapid shake-up that comes alongside a public pledge of loyalty to her from the military high command, according to images released by the Miraflores presidential palace and distributed by Reuters [E001][E002][E003].

Photographs from Miraflores Palace show Rodríguez presiding over a ceremony in which senior officers formally swore loyalty to her leadership [E002][E001]. Reuters reports that this came after she announced the dismissal of the defence minister and the replacement of Venezuela’s top military commanders [E003].

The sequence marks one of her first major institutional moves since assuming the interim presidency.

While full details of each new appointee were not set out in the available material, the core development reported is that the previous senior command, including a defence minister described as close to Maduro, has been removed, and a new leadership slate has been installed under Rodríguez’s direction [E003].

The fact that the military has now sworn loyalty to Rodríguez is presented in the reporting as a significant signal that she is consolidating authority at a moment of transition [E001][E002].

The dismissal of a defence minister characterized in the Guardian’s reporting as close to Maduro further highlights the political weight of the move [E003].

According to the account carried with the Miraflores Palace photographs, Rodríguez’s announcement came in two steps: first, the firing of the defence minister; second, the broader replacement of the top military commanders [E003]. The loyalty ceremony followed, with the new leadership appearing alongside her in the presidential palace [E002].

However, the description that she has “replaced Venezuela’s top military commanders” indicates that the changes reach the highest levels of the armed forces’ hierarchy [E003].

The emphasis in the official imagery is on unity and continuity of state authority under Rodríguez, rather than on the individual profiles of the new officers. The photographs show a tightly choreographed event, with the interim president at the center and the military leadership arrayed around her as they swear loyalty [E002].

### How this fits into a wider pattern of military-led resets

While the Venezuelan situation is distinct, the use of rapid personnel changes and loyalty tests to reset political control over state institutions has parallels elsewhere. In Madagascar, for example, the country’s military ruler Michael Randrianirina recently dismissed the prime minister and cabinet without public explanation and announced that a new cabinet would be named early next week [E004][E006].

He has also decreed that future ministers must pass lie detector tests, a measure he claims is aimed at combating corruption [E006].

Photographs from that episode show Randrianirina addressing the nation as he outlines the new requirements and the forthcoming cabinet announcement [E005][E006].

These comparisons do not imply identical circumstances, but they illustrate a broader trend in which leaders facing political flux move quickly to reshape the top tiers of the security and executive apparatus, often accompanied by highly staged public events to demonstrate loyalty and compliance.

For Venezuela, the central fact at this stage is that the armed forces’ top command has been reshuffled under Delcy Rodríguez, and that the military has publicly sworn loyalty to her interim presidency [E001][E002][E003].

Key questions that remain open, based on the limited detail in the reporting, include:

- The identities and backgrounds of the newly appointed commanders, which may shape how the military behaves under Rodríguez’s leadership. - Whether the loyalty pledge translates into consistent support in the event of political disputes or challenges to her authority. - How the removal of a defence minister close to Maduro may affect internal alignments within the ruling elite and the officer corps [E003].

For now, the clearest takeaway from the available reporting is that Rodríguez has moved quickly to secure control over the armed forces’ top command.

The images from Miraflores Palace, showing senior officers swearing loyalty to her, are intended to signal that the armed forces are, at least for the moment, aligned with her interim presidency [E001][E002].
