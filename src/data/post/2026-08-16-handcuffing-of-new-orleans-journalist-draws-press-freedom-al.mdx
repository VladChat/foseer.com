A New Orleans journalist was briefly handcuffed in July while covering the criminal indictment of Louisiana attorney general Liz Murrill after he protested the closure of a courtroom to the public, according to a report by the Guardian. A national media advocacy group described the incident as “outrageous” and warned it sends a “chilling message” to reporters covering the courts.

The case against Murrill was later dismissed, the Guardian reported, but press advocates say the treatment of the journalist raises broader concerns about access to judicial proceedings and the handling of reporters who challenge restrictions.

## What happened in the New Orleans courtroom

The Guardian reported that New Orleans journalist Danny Monteverde was at the courthouse in July to cover the indictment proceedings against Murrill, the state’s attorney general. When the courtroom was closed during the indictment, Monteverde protested the decision, arguing that the proceeding should remain open to the public and the press.

According to the Guardian’s account, officials responded by placing Monteverde in handcuffs. He was restrained inside the courthouse in connection with his protest over the closure, the outlet reported. The article did not indicate that he was charged with a crime, and it did not describe any allegations of violence or threats on his part.

The incident occurred during a high-profile moment for the state’s top law enforcement official, adding to the sensitivity around both the indictment and the conditions under which it was conducted, the Guardian reported.

## Media group calls action ‘outrageous’

A media advocacy organization quoted by the Guardian condemned the handcuffing as “outrageous” and said it sends a “chilling message” to journalists who seek to assert their right to observe court proceedings.

The group argued that restraining a reporter in this context could discourage other journalists from challenging courtroom closures or access limits, according to the Guardian’s reporting. The organization framed the incident as a press freedom concern tied directly to the public’s ability to monitor the justice system.

The Guardian did not report any response from court officials or law enforcement addressing the advocacy group’s criticism, and it did not indicate whether any internal review of the incident had been announced.

## Indictment of attorney general later dismissed

The Guardian reported that the criminal case against Murrill, which Monteverde had been assigned to cover, was ultimately dismissed after the July indictment. Details about the grounds for dismissal were not included in the account used for this report.

The dismissal means the underlying criminal allegations no longer pose an immediate legal threat to the attorney general, but the circumstances of the indictment and the treatment of the press during that proceeding remain a point of contention for media advocates, according to the Guardian.

## Why the incident matters

Press freedom advocates quoted by the Guardian say the New Orleans episode illustrates how quickly tensions can escalate when reporters contest limits on access to judicial proceedings. They argue that handcuffing a working journalist in the course of such a dispute risks deterring robust coverage of sensitive legal cases.

The Guardian’s reporting also notes that the incident unfolded around the indictment of a sitting state attorney general, a position that oversees major law enforcement and legal decisions. That context has heightened concern among media groups about how officials respond when reporters press for transparency in politically charged cases.

## Calls for closer scrutiny

The Guardian’s account has prompted questions from press advocates about how courtroom access decisions are made and enforced, and how journalists are treated when they challenge those decisions. The media group quoted in the report urged authorities to examine the circumstances that led to Monteverde being handcuffed.

Independent corroboration of specific details beyond the Guardian’s reporting is limited at this stage and will bear watching as additional public records or statements emerge. For now, the incident stands as a closely watched example for press freedom organizations monitoring interactions between journalists, courts and law enforcement.

The episode’s outcome — a dismissed indictment and a journalist briefly restrained while protesting closed doors — has become a focal point in ongoing discussions about transparency in high-profile legal proceedings and the boundaries of acceptable official responses to press scrutiny.
