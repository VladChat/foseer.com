Shippers and industry insiders are pushing back against Donald Trump’s proposal to charge a 20% fee on cargo transiting the Strait of Hormuz, calling the idea legally dubious and potentially destabilizing for global trade.

The concerns were detailed in reporting by CBS News on May 10, which quoted shipping and financial industry figures describing the suggested levy as a “dangerous Pandora’s box” and saying there is “no legal basis” for such a charge under current international norms.

## What Trump Proposed

According to CBS News, Trump has floated the idea of imposing a 20% fee on cargo moving through the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway that carries a significant share of the world’s seaborne oil. The report describes the proposal as a charge that could apply to ships using the passage, potentially adding tens of millions of dollars in costs per vessel on some voyages.

CBS News reports that the suggested fee is framed as a way for the United States to be compensated for the security it provides in and around the strait. The report does not describe any formal policy document or enacted measure, but rather a proposal attributed to Trump and discussed among affected stakeholders.

Industry sources cited by CBS News say that, at the levels discussed, the fee could quickly reach into the tens of millions of dollars for large crude carriers or container ships, depending on cargo value and routing. Those figures are presented as estimates by people familiar with shipping economics, not as official U.S. government calculations.

## Why Shippers Are Alarmed

Maritime industry insiders quoted by CBS News argue that there is no established legal authority for the United States to unilaterally impose such a fee on international shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. They describe the proposal as opening a “dangerous Pandora’s box,” warning that if one country claimed the right to charge for passage through a critical chokepoint, others might follow.

The same insiders tell CBS News they fear the move could undermine long-standing principles of freedom of navigation, a core concept in maritime law that governs how commercial vessels move through international waters and key straits. They also warn that sudden, large new costs could disrupt shipping contracts and insurance arrangements that were negotiated without any expectation of a 20% transit charge.

CBS News reports that some in the shipping community view the idea as not only commercially risky but also potentially destabilizing, because it could invite disputes over who controls access to the strait and on what terms. These concerns are presented as the views of industry participants, not as legal rulings or official positions from governments.

## Financial Sector and Corporate Concerns

The CBS News report notes that major financial institutions, including JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs, are among those watching the situation because of their exposure to global shipping, energy markets, and related financing. While the report does not quote specific executives from these firms, it identifies them as institutions with a stake in how any new fee regime might affect trade flows and asset values.

People familiar with the matter, cited by CBS News, say banks and investors are focused on the potential for higher transit costs to ripple through commodity prices and shipping rates. For large cargoes, the report notes, a 20% fee could translate into tens of millions of dollars per ship, a scale of cost that could affect how routes are chosen and how deals are structured.

CBS News also links Israel to the unfolding discussion, reporting that Israeli interests are among those tracking the proposal’s implications. The report does not provide detailed public statements from Israeli officials but indicates that the country’s strategic and economic interests in regional shipping lanes make the issue relevant to its planning.

## Legal and Diplomatic Questions

Industry figures quoted by CBS News repeatedly stress that, in their view, there is “no legal basis” for the United States to impose a unilateral 20% fee on cargo transiting the Strait of Hormuz. They point to existing international norms that treat key straits as shared routes for global commerce, though the report does not delve into specific treaty articles or court decisions.

The CBS News account emphasizes that these are assessments from shippers and related insiders, not binding legal opinions. It also notes that independent corroboration of some details remains limited at this stage and should be monitored as more reporting emerges.

Diplomatic implications are only briefly touched on in the CBS News reporting. The article indicates that any attempt to change the financial terms of passage through such a critical chokepoint could draw reactions from governments that rely on the route for energy exports and imports. However, the report does not describe any formal diplomatic responses or policy shifts tied directly to Trump’s proposal.

## What to Watch Next

Based on the CBS News account, the proposal to impose a 20% fee on cargo through the Strait of Hormuz remains at the level of a floated idea rather than an implemented policy. Shippers, financial institutions such as JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs, and interested governments, including Israel, are portrayed as closely watching for any concrete steps that might follow.

Industry insiders quoted in the CBS report say they are concerned about both the immediate cost impact—potentially tens of millions of dollars per ship—and the broader precedent that such a fee could set. They argue that how this proposal is handled could influence future debates over who pays for security in strategic waterways.

For now, the CBS News reporting underscores two central points: key players in global shipping see no clear legal foundation for the proposed fee, and they fear that moving ahead with it could unsettle established rules that underpin international maritime trade.
