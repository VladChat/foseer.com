Roberto De Zerbi has said Tottenham Hotspur must “bring in top players” in the current transfer window after escaping a relegation battle, describing the club’s situation as too fragile to rely on tactical improvements alone.

In an interview published by the New York Times, the Tottenham head coach discussed the state of his squad, the pressure of avoiding the drop, and his discomfort at being publicly praised by Manchester City manager Pep Guardiola.

## De Zerbi: Tottenham need higher-calibre signings

De Zerbi told the New York Times that Tottenham “need to bring in top players” to compete at the level the club expects. His comments came in the context of a discussion about the recently completed season, in which Spurs avoided relegation but did not meet their previous ambitions of challenging near the top of the Premier League table, according to the paper’s account of the interview.

The coach’s remarks, as reported by the New York Times, focused on the quality gap he sees between Tottenham and the league’s leading sides. While the article did not list specific transfer targets, it described De Zerbi as clear that the current squad requires significant reinforcement rather than minor adjustments.

By framing the issue as one of needing “top players,” De Zerbi signalled that he is looking beyond depth signings or long-term prospects and toward additions who can immediately raise the team’s level. The New York Times report did not include a response from Tottenham’s ownership or recruitment department to those comments.

## Reflecting on a season spent avoiding relegation

The interview also addressed Tottenham’s struggle to stay clear of the relegation zone. The New York Times reported that De Zerbi spoke about “escaping relegation,” a phrase that underlined how far the club’s short-term reality has drifted from its recent years of regular European qualification.

While the article did not provide a full statistical breakdown of the season, it presented De Zerbi’s own characterisation of the campaign as one in which survival, rather than silverware or Champions League qualification, became the primary concern. That framing, attributed directly to the coach, helped explain the urgency behind his call for higher-level signings.

According to the New York Times account, De Zerbi did not blame individual players in public. Instead, he described the overall level of the squad as insufficient for the ambitions typically associated with Tottenham. His comments positioned the transfer window as a turning point: either the club upgrades its talent base or risks facing similar struggles again.

## Embarrassed by Guardiola’s praise

Alongside his assessment of Tottenham’s needs, De Zerbi addressed praise he has received from Pep Guardiola. The New York Times reported that the Manchester City manager has spoken positively about De Zerbi’s work, but the Tottenham coach said he feels “embarrassed” by that level of public admiration.

The article described De Zerbi as respectful of Guardiola’s record while also wary of being elevated by association when his own team has been fighting to avoid relegation. By using the word “embarrassed,” as quoted in the New York Times, De Zerbi signalled that he does not believe praise from one of the game’s most successful managers should overshadow Tottenham’s current on-pitch problems.

The report did not quote Guardiola in the same piece, instead summarising his earlier positive remarks about De Zerbi’s style of play and coaching ideas. Within that context, De Zerbi’s reaction underscored his view that external compliments are less important than concrete improvements to results and squad quality.

## Why De Zerbi’s comments matter for Tottenham

Although the New York Times interview focused on De Zerbi’s perspective rather than club policy, his public insistence that Tottenham must sign “top players” places clear expectations on the current transfer window.

From a football-operations standpoint, a head coach openly calling for higher-calibre recruits, as reported by a major outlet, can shape how supporters and media evaluate the club’s actions in the market. If Spurs do not secure the level of signings De Zerbi believes are necessary, his remarks may be revisited as a benchmark for what he felt was required after a season spent avoiding relegation.

At the same time, the interview, as recounted by the New York Times, showed De Zerbi trying to balance external praise with internal urgency. His discomfort at Guardiola’s compliments, alongside his blunt assessment of Tottenham’s squad needs, highlighted a coach who sees a gap between how he is sometimes perceived and the realities of his team’s league position.

The New York Times report did not outline specific next steps from the club or provide detailed information on ongoing negotiations. For now, De Zerbi’s comments stand as a clear, on-the-record signal of what he believes Tottenham must do: use this transfer window to add players capable of immediately lifting the team above relegation battles and closer to the level of the league’s leading sides.

## What to watch next

The key developments to monitor, based on the priorities De Zerbi laid out in his New York Times interview, are straightforward:

- Which new signings Tottenham complete in the current transfer window, and whether they fit the “top players” standard the coach described.
- How the club’s early-season performances compare with the relegation-threatened campaign De Zerbi referenced.
- Whether De Zerbi’s public stance on recruitment prompts any visible shift in how Tottenham’s hierarchy discusses its squad-building strategy.

Until more information emerges from the club or additional reporting, De Zerbi’s interview offers the clearest available view of how Tottenham’s head coach sees the team’s recent struggles and what he believes is required to move beyond them.
